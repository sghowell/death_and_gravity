# Smoothness on the same slab: a flat-start Picard argument

The generic A.10 inverse is not a `C1` endomorphism. In particular its
image of a nonzero constant has a singular initial derivative. This
proof uses the actual smooth common past and an unchanged **initial
neighborhood**, not that false endomorphism assertion.

## 1. Causal convolution with a flat input

Let `K` be the normalized pole-plus-cut kernel of
`J_gammaE/(4pi^2)`. A.10 proves `K in L1[0,L]`, with positive mass at
most the bound `k` in the new contraction proof. If a smooth function
`f` is zero on a fixed initial neighborhood, extend it by zero before
0. For every finite `m`, differentiating the translated smooth input
under the integrable kernel gives

    (K*f)^(m)=K*f^(m), ||K*f^(m)|| <= k ||f^(m)||.                (6)

The same conclusion follows after any smooth extension past the final
endpoint; convolution at earlier times does not depend on that choice.
There is no differentiated singular kernel and no lower endpoint term.

Start the full-map Picard iteration with `X0=0`. Every iterate is zero
on `[0,L/4]`: on that interval `c=cbar`, and the metric, auxiliary
solution and complete mode histories in (4) agree. Causality of the
map preserves this common zero interval. It also ensures that the
potential is glued to the fixed past with all jets unchanged.

## 2. All-order time transfer for the actual nonlinear modes

For the actual Dyson terms of A.10, write `R_n[u]` for the homogeneous
degree-`n` contribution. At each finite order it is a continuous
multilinear integral in copies of `u`. For `n>=3`, its operator norm
is bounded by `c_n` from the absolutely convergent IR/UV audit. For
`n=2`, use the separately polarized logarithmic triangle; its kernel
mass is `t^2/2`. It must not be replaced by the divergent absolute UV
bound at order two.

The time-transfer identity of A.10 can be iterated when every jet of
`u` vanishes at the original free join. Integration by parts at each
step then has a zero original endpoint. Equivalently the quadratic
integral can be written in lags `0<=v<=w<=t`, with integrand
`u(t-v)u(t-w) log(w/(w-v))`; its moving far-end boundary contains an
original vanishing jet. The mode-chain transfer has the identical
property. Hence

    d^m/dt^m Rmode'[u]
        = D Rmode[u](u^(m+1)) + L_m[u],                         (7)

where, for `m>=1`, `L_m` involves only `u` through derivative order
`m`. At order `n`, derivatives are distributed among `n` potential
factors by the ordinary multinomial rule. There are exactly `n`
terms containing the single highest derivative; all other terms
contain lower derivatives. For each fixed `m`, the extra combinatorial
factor grows only polynomially in `n` and the factorial Dyson majorant
still sums. Bounded lower jets therefore give bounded, continuous
lower-jet remainders, uniformly in time. The same majorants justify
interchanging frequency integrals, sums and these finite derivatives.

Here `D Rmode[u]` denotes the derivative with respect to a potential
direction `Z` in the **uniform potential norm**, not the A.10 derivative
norm. If `||u||<=B=MT` and `z=MT^3`, polarization yields

    ||D R_2[u] Z|| <= B T^2 ||Z|| = z ||Z||,
    ||sum_(n>=3) D R_n[u] Z||
           <= 18 z^2 exp(2z) ||Z||.                            (8)

The latter uses the larger already-proved sum with `n^2` in place of
`n`. These operators are continuous in `u` in operator norm. More
explicitly, the quadratic second derivative has norm at most `T^2`.
At `n>=3`, polarizing again and putting `j=n-2>=1` gives

    sum n(n-1)c_n B^(n-2)
       =4T^2 sum_(j>=1) (2z)^j/j! [1/2+1/j]
       <=6T^2[exp(2z)-1].

Consequently `||DR[u]-DR[v]|| <= T^2[1+6(exp(2z)-1)] ||u-v||`
on the amplitude ball. Neither the bound nor its continuity requires
numerical control of all jets.

## 3. The highest-jet coefficient is uniform in derivative order

Let `a_n,q_n` be the metric and auxiliary data generated by `X_n`.
Differentiating the full Picard map `m` times and using (6)--(7) gives

    X_(n+1)^(m) = A_n X_n^(m) + B_(m,n),
    A_n Z = -K*[ d(a_n) Z + D Rmode[u_n] Z ].                    (9)

Directions in the displayed `D Rmode` are extended by zero into the
common past. The fixed baseline derivatives contribute to `B_(m,n)`.
All other terms in (4) belong to that lower-jet remainder: `W` gains
one derivative from `X`, `h` gains two, `a` gains three, and `q'`
gains two by the integrated equation (3) with smooth prescribed `c`.
For the first two derivative orders this statement is read directly
from the integral/ODE formulas, without negative derivative notation.
In particular the factor `1/(60delta)` is present in the remainder;
it is finite and does not multiply the highest `X^(m)`.

The caps of the full ball give `z=27/100000`, `|d|<=17/30` and
`k<21/100`. Therefore the same number

    q_smooth = k[17/30 + z + 18z^2/(1-2z)] < 3/25 < 1           (10)

bounds every `A_n`, independently of `m`. The map `u -> A_n` is
operator-norm continuous on the ball. The lower remainder `B_(m,n)`
is uniformly bounded if the preceding jets are uniformly bounded,
and converges uniformly if they converge uniformly. This follows
from the finite ODE/integral compositions and the polarized Dyson
majorants just described. Cutoff derivatives may enter these bounds
at arbitrarily high orders, but for each fixed order they are finite;
no order-independent bound on all metric jets is asserted.

## 4. Uniform bounds and convergence in every finite smooth norm

The full contraction gives convergence of `X_n` in `C0` and keeps
every iterate in the certified ball. Assume inductively convergence
through derivative order `m-1`. The initial iterate is smooth.
Equation (9) implies

    ||X_(n+1)^(m)|| <= q_smooth ||X_n^(m)|| + C_m,

so the `m`th derivatives are uniformly bounded. Comparing two
iterates, the operator-norm continuity of `A_n`, the lower-jet
convergence, and that uniform bound give

    ||X_(n+1)^(m)-X_(j+1)^(m)||
       <= q_smooth ||X_n^(m)-X_j^(m)|| + e_(n,j),
    e_(n,j) -> 0 as min(n,j) -> infinity.

Take the decreasing supremum over sufficiently late pairs, then its
limit. It is finite by the uniform bound and is at most `q_smooth`
times itself. It must be zero. Thus the `m`th derivatives are Cauchy
uniformly. Uniform derivative convergence identifies the limit as
the derivative of the preceding limit. This completes the induction
for every finite `m`, on the same fixed interval `[0,L]`.

Consequently the fixed point is smooth and vanishes on the same initial
neighborhood; `a` is smooth and positive. The old actual state is
transported along this smooth geometry from its unchanged Hadamard
past, rather than interpreted through arbitrary formal state moments.
Its Wick function agrees with the smooth actual-mode functional in
(7). The full equation and constraint can now be evaluated pointwise.

This argument does not give analytic/Gevrey estimates, a uniform bound
on every higher jet, or large-interval stability. It also does not
apply to arbitrary finite-regularity state data lacking the common
smooth flat history and forcing used here.
