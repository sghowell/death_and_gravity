# The certified interval does not close the cosmological focusing gate

Write T0=A eta_star^2 and tau_hat=tau/T0. The available proper span is
at most `D=3L/2=3/(2*10^10)`. This conclusion is about the comoving
normals to constant cosmic-time Cauchy surfaces, the congruence for which
the field bound is uniform. It is not an exclusion for other hypersurfaces.

In the FK convention the actual Ricci component is

    R_UU T0^2=-3(u+h^2)/a^2,
    |R_UU| T0^2<=3(2*10^-12+1/4)/4<1/4,
    |K|T0=3h/a<=3/4.

Future expansion is positive; reversing the normal reverses K. In either
orientation, for any segment wholly inside the certified free half and
any admissible index trial g with g(0)=1, g(tau)=0, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    integral g^2 <= (tau^2/2) integral g'^2,
    integral g'^2 >=1/tau.

The first inequality follows from
`|g(t)|^2<= (tau-t) integral_t^tau g'^2` and integration; it does not
assume that g lies between zero and one. Consequently the actual index
form used by FK obeys

    J[g] T0 >= [3-tau_hat^2/8]/tau_hat
              =3/tau_hat-tau_hat/8 >=3/D-D/8 >3/4 >=-K T0.

All coefficients and strict margins are checked exactly. Thus the
index-form sufficient focusing condition `J[g]<=-K` cannot be met on
these segments, even if one uses actual curvature instead of its QSEI
upper bound. This is stronger than merely observing that a rounded QSEI
constant is large. Consistently, the normal Jacobi volume is proportional
to a^3 and its ratio is >=(2/3)^3=8/27, so it has no zero inside the slab.

For an additional scale comparison, ordinary radiation has nonnegative
EED. The new QSEI and the actual SEE therefore give a geometric
coefficient `Q2=kappa*2*hbar/(16pi^2)=360 delta T0^2`, with Q0=0.
The available interval has

    Q2/tau_available^2 >=360delta/D^2=160000000.

This is the ratio for the sufficient coefficient 2, not a lower bound on
all possible optimal field constants. Choosing a coefficient closer to
one would not repair the actual index-form obstruction above.

FK's global implication also needs the required domain of normal
geodesics and initial contraction, not just a local energy inequality.
We have not constructed the long continuation necessary for a
cosmological application. A smooth interval with a>0 does not become a
cosmological singularity simply by declaring its artificial endpoint to
be the edge of the spacetime. No maximal-extension or incompleteness
conclusion is made here. Massive/interacting fields and the other
original P8(a) hypotheses remain separate research obligations.
