# Falsifiable next step, not an inferred nonlinear instability

Resumming the Einstein coefficient is algebraically possible and the
resulting inverse is causal. It nevertheless contains a very fast positive
pole. The exact lower norms rule out one specific shortcut: replacing the
entire preconditioned equation by an assumed uniformly small C0 inverse on
a macroscopic interval. They do not rule out continuation of a special
actual solution, a different norm with appropriate geometric barriers, or
state/geometry correlations which suppress the unstable projection.

The next concrete analysis is to keep the exact full remainder Gf from
notes/decomposition.md and prove, on an interval longer than A.11's,

1. A validated baseline continuation, with the same original state and
   source-free density constraint, and a numerical bound for its actual
   Wick-map residual r_b. The existing A.8 surrogate is not automatically
   a compatible post-A.11 continuation.
2. A weighted Lipschitz and self-map estimate for Gf, including the actual
   state derivative R'[u]-R'[ub], the coefficient mismatch divided by
   delta, all q terms and the anomaly. The inequality
   `(240/769)*Lip_sigma<1` is only one test; pointwise positivity of a,
   fixed-history state compatibility and a self-map barrier must still
   hold after undoing the weight. A weighted small ball can be enormous
   in the physical supremum norm.
3. A bound for the growing-pole numerator of the full residual/history
   forcing. For finite trial intervals this is a retarded integral, not
   a license to impose a boundary condition in an unknown future. Either
   prove a useful suppression estimate or demonstrate that the proposed
   baseline/error budget cannot meet one. The frozen pole alone does not
   decide that question.

One can falsify a proposed quantitative continuation certificate by checking
these exact residual and projection estimates. The current checkpoint
contains no such rolling bounds and no claim that the actual A.11 solution
excites its comparator's pole. In particular it does not identify a
generic nonlinear no-go or a maximum allowable local step.

The original focusing objective still requires adequate exact-solution
duration and normal-geodesic coverage, contraction data, and an energy
inequality for realistic fields. A.12 covers a very short interval for one
massless minimal field. Neither this pole calculation nor an artificial
endpoint provides cosmological incompleteness. Choosing a no-runaway or
order-reduced physical branch would be a separate explicit decision;
none is silently made here.
