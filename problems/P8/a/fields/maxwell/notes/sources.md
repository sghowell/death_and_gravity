## Primary field and renormalization sources

- [Fewster–Pfenning, gr-qc/0303106](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0303106):
  theorem V.1 and its local positive-type proof, equation 84, and the
  explicit Maxwell flat-vacuum calculation 97–102. Target states are
  Hadamard. The general construction has compact-Cauchy/topological
  hypotheses; the Minkowski section explicitly dispenses with them.
  Our conformal-strip proof uses the restricted flat kernel and local
  smearings, not an unstated global extension of every target state.
- [Pfenning, gr-qc/0107075](https://arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0107075), equation 97:
  Maxwell's flat inertial coefficient is hbar/(8pi^2) times the squared
  second derivative of the square-root weight. We write that real sampler
  as f, so the averaging weight is f^2. This is not an optimality claim.
- [Herzog–Huang, 1301.5002v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.5002), introduction,
  equation 23 and footnote 2: type-D-free scheme, conformal-vacuum stress,
  and conformal transformation law. Equations 25,27,28 fix its physical
  energy sign versus mixed time component. The explicit Lorentzian
  HH-to-FK dictionary is in the proof. Adding a finite R^2 variation
  changes the total-derivative anomaly and is a new Maxwell parameter,
  not a scalar gamma choice.
- [Markowicz et al., 2006.06884](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.06884),
  equations 37–39 and 47–48: the Maxwell anomaly b=-62, conformal weight
  and quadratic H3 tensor. Their R_source=-6a''/a^3 is opposite FK.
  Their conductor/Casimir examples are not our boundary conditions.
  We do not use their printed derivative H1 tensor to normalize beta_M:
  literal FLRW conversion has the conservation discrepancy documented
  in the proof. Our finite-beta tensor is explicitly defined by the
  conserved FK variation and checked independently. This is a precise
  non-import boundary, not a broad judgement about that paper.
