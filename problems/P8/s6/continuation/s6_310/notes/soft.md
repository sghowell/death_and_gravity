# Two distinct leading soft limits

Write J(epsilon,q;state)=sum_p p.epsilon.p/(p.q), with all hard
momenta outgoing. The metric convention supplies1/sqrt(kappa) for
each leading soft factor.

First fix a generic nonforward elastic hard state and two fixed
noncollinear null directions, and scale q1,q2 to zero at the same
rate lambda. The only lambda^-2 tree contributions have two
near-on-shell external hard propagators. Emissions on different
legs factor directly. On one leg the two orders combine using

1/[a(a+b)]+1/[b(a+b)]=1/(ab), a=p.q1, b=p.q2.

Scalar double-metric seagulls have only one such hard pole.
A purely soft internal graviton has propagator O(lambda^-2), but
its cubic Einstein vertex is O(lambda^2); with its single hard-leg
soft pole it is only O(lambda^-1). All hard internal propagators
are bounded at the fixed generic kinematics. Hence the leading
limit is M6=A_B J1 J2/kappa+O(lambda^-1), where A_B is the full
original elastic Born. The error here is a pointwise soft order;
its coefficient is not asserted uniform near collinear or forward
boundaries.

Second hold one emitted graviton q2 fixed as part of the hard
radiative state and take q1 soft. The leading tree theorem sums
over every external hard particle, including that null graviton:
M6=M5 J(epsilon1,q1;{p1,p2,p3,p4,q2})/sqrt(kappa)+O(1).
The leading external pole couples to hard momentum independently
of spin. Spin-connection and derivative-soft terms carry an extra
soft momentum and enter the next order. The cubic Einstein vertex
provides the q2-leg residue; removing it is not the leading
theorem for this external state. The full current is conserved:
J_tensor eta q1=sum_hard p=0. The massive-only current instead has
divergence -q2.

These fixed-kinematics tree statements agree with the primary
[Chakrabarti et al. multiple-soft analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.06803v2),
especially its leading external-pole counting and its restriction
to trees in four dimensions. Its subleading formula also contains
a two-soft contact term; a product of leading factors is not the
complete nonleading amplitude. No all-loop four-dimensional
theorem is imported here.

Two exact rational recoil families test these normalizations at
the actual original kappa,n,g,C. The simultaneous family uses
E=5/4, alpha=2-h, Eprime=(alpha+1/alpha)/2, opposite equal-energy
null rays and a fixed rational outgoing scalar direction.
The hierarchical family holds q2 fixed, uses alpha=19/10-h and
solves the exact invariant pair mass for the other null energy.
All shells/conservation and total-energy limits hold exactly.
At h=1/100,1/1000,1/10000 both correct normalized ratios approach1;
the last errors are below1/100 and strictly decreasing.
The massive-only hierarchical normalization instead approaches
about0.95027, and its last error is over ten times the correct one.

These are independent calibrations at finite h. The existence and
orders of the stated limits come from the preceding tree pole
argument. No finite sample supplies a bound on their integrated
nonleading remainder or permits interchanging forward, collinear,
threshold and regulator limits.
