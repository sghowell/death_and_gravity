# The slope's epsilon coefficient contributes to the finite reference

The complete insertion integrand is
V1 V2(Q1+Q2)/(2 D1 D2), with Qi=alpha_D+Ri.
Its constant part is twice alpha_D times the original one-loop
bubble integrand. With I0bar the entire zero-momentum bubble,
the old interaction counterterm is

    -alpha_D I0bar sum_channels C(z)^2.

The interaction-MS counterterm removes only its pole,
-alpha_0 sum C^2/(Q epsilon). The finite difference is therefore

    F_alpha sum C^2,
    F_alpha=Fin[alpha_D I0bar]
           =[alpha_0 ell+alpha_1]/Q
           =(g/Q^2) integral b[2ell-logD] dx.

Here I0bar=[1/epsilon+ell+O(epsilon)]/Q. The alpha_1 term
is essential: replacing alpha_D by alpha_0 before multiplying
by the pole loses alpha_1/Q. Both occurrences of the scale
come from one common dimensional convention, not from
adding the first-order scale change twice.

All outer vertex choices and both insertion positions remain;
the two convergent R insertions are unchanged. Their dimensional
difference has no remaining pole that could turn an evanescent
term into another local contribution. Any allocation of the
fixed momentum-independent potential contact has zero b2.

This completes the assigned outer interaction-reference
conversion, not the separate global field/coupling map.
