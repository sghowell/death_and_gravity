# What this fixes and what still has to be matched

S286 gave the complete generated massive matter F1 and a conditional
background charge identity for internal gravity. This successor
supplies the missing ordinary/background and spacelike-continuity
bridge in the specified pure-GR one-loop sector.

It therefore removes an independent zero-transfer proper-endpoint
charge renormalization after the same scalar external-leg factors.
It is useful input to physical pole factorization, but it is not itself
the full pole-factorization calculation. In particular:

- S285's light-Phi c_R, c_W and c_R2 remain unmatched. Neither the
  physical Newton residue nor its sign follows from the fixed H/Proca
  subkernel bound.
- S284's physical graviton double poles and the remaining tadpole/local
  rational completion must still be checked in the complete amplitude.
  This proof does not simply erase those terms or assert that every
  double pole is a cosmological tadpole.
- The finite F1 slope is not determined by the zero-transfer Ward
  identity. S286's explicit transverse Ricci-derivative addition leaves
  the scalar inverse and F1(0) unchanged while shifting the slope.
  Full F2, H-metric mixing and curved improvements remain.
- The finite crossed amplitude, improved low-cut subtraction/b20,
  physical infrared prescription and finite Regge contour remainder
  require their own calculations. No omitted-loop bound is supplied.

## Comparison with primary literature

The background-field Ward framework is consistent with the
[Armendariz-Picon and Penco analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1108.6028).
That argument is not imported as an automatic ordinary-vertex or
four-dimensional infrared-continuity proof.

The scalar form-factor nonanalytic terms in
[Bjerrum-Bohr, Donoghue and Holstein](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0211071)
are compatible with a continuous F1 and a potentially singular F2.
A nonanalytic subset or potential/Born subtraction is not used to
replace this complete proper-vertex topology/domain argument.

The infrared restrictions in
[Sen's all-order soft analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/1703.00024)
and the loop-limit qualifications in
[Bern, Davies and Nohle](https://arxiv.org/html/1405.1015)
are retained, not bypassed by setting t=0 before examining the loop.
No higher-dimensional IR-finite theorem is treated as a ready-made
D4 scattering or exact-state result.

These comparisons support scope and conventions; the load-bearing
algebra and elementary bounds are stated and checked locally.
