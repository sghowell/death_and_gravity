# A polynomial integrand is not an integrated local polynomial

The Taylor polynomial is formed at fixed real k on the high band |k|>=m. Its coefficients are analytic derivatives of the unregulated unit-W8 endpoint row. The original cutoff chi_K(P,k)=1_(|k|<=K,|-k+P|<=K) depends on external P. It is not Taylor-differentiated or assumed analytic.

A simple exact geometric counterexample prevents a false locality inference. Integrating the constant polynomial1 over the intersection of two radiusK balls separated by d=|P| gives, for0<=d<=2K,

V_K(P)=pi(4K+d)(2K-d)^2/12
      =4pi K^3/3-pi K^2|P|+pi|P|^3/12.

The one-sided derivatives along a signed spatial axis at zero are opposite, with jump-2pi K^2. Thus a polynomial integrand can acquire nonanalytic external dependence through the moving sharp domain. The leading surface artifact in this fixture is proportional toK^2|P|.

This fixture is NOT a computation of an actual Proca divergence coefficient, nor proof that such a term survives cancellations in the full current. It establishes only that locality and cancellation cannot be assumed from the integrand polynomial alone.

Accordingly P_K is called the Taylor-polynomial INTEGRAND sector, not an already local counterterm. Its regulator artifacts, finite and divergent coefficients and combination with C_unit,K must be handled in the original fixed covariant prescription. In particular, the full contact's one-leg band cannot be silently replaced by the memory's two-leg band.

The convergent Q remainder does not need that unresolved matching: its dominated limit is already independent of this cutoff removal. But Q is not the full response. No physical cutoff or replacement renormalization scheme has been selected, and no higher-order finite Taylor oversubtraction is dropped.
