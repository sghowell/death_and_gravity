# Complete tracefree spatial space and nonsingular momentum maps

For P along the third axis, an orthonormal Frobenius basis consists of

diag(1,-1,0)/sqrt(2), (e12+e21)/sqrt(2),
(e13+e31)/sqrt(2), (e23+e32)/sqrt(2),
diag(-1,-1,2)/sqrt(6).

These are two tensor directions, two vector directions and one scalar shear. Their names refer only to rotations about P, not to lapse/shift-constrained propagating modes.

The exact polynomial maps B_P(M)=TF sym(P tensor MP) and C_P(M)=TF(P tensor P)(P.M.P) have eigenvalues

B: (0,0,q/2,q/2,2q/3),
C: (0,0,0,0,2q^2/3).

No inverse power of |P| appears in their definition. At zero momentum both maps vanish and all five directions have the same zero-momentum limit. A basis chosen relative to nonzero P is used for estimation, not to introduce a singular operator.

The Einstein spatial coefficients c are(1,1,0,0,-1/3). The Weyl operators are partial_eta^4+b q partial_eta^2+d q^2, with

b=(2,2,1,1,2/3),
d=(1,1,0,0,1/9).

The R-squared algebraic coefficient e is(0,0,0,0,2/3). Both vector directions and the scalar gradient sign are retained. Neither a vanishing vector Einstein spatial term nor the scalar shear sign is interpreted as a physical stability verdict before constraints are solved.

As a separate full-tensor check, take w=omega^2, z=w-q and A=zI+PP^T. The full spatial Weyl Hessian is TF[A M A-A tr(AM)/3], while the R-squared Hessian is2TF(A)tr(AM). The polynomial maps reproduce these complete covariant curvature polynomials for arbitrary P and tracefree M, not just a single transverse projection. This comparison does not replace the original finite coefficients with ultraviolet cut weights.
