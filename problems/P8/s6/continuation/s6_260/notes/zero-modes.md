# Translation ghosts and finite projection are separate boundaries

On the torus write c=c0+cperp, with c0=P0c the mean. The full
differential is

sc0=P0(c dot grad c),
scperp=(1-P0)(c dot grad c).

The first term is not generally zero when c0=0. For independent odd
constants e1,e2 and c^1=e1 sin x+e2 cos x, the generated ghost is
c^1 partial_x c^1=-e1 e2, a nonzero translation. If one deletes that
constant and uses only the projected rule, then a scalar has

s_projected^2 f=e1 e2 partial_x f.

This is an explicit nilpotency defect, not just the existence of a
linear ghost zero mode. Retain the entire translation group or an
explicit residual-group prescription. Dividing a group volume for
invariant observables does not define arbitrary coordinate sources
without also transporting or fixing them.

The S258 local mean-displacement slice remains useful. It supplies
a complement for the implicit-function theorem, not a Lie subalgebra.
Its finite-torus inverse has the stated L-dependent infrared norm.
There is no global slice, inverse on translations or uniform
infinite-volume bound here.

A separate exact truncated-Witt calculation projects the bracket
of e^(imx) partial_x to |m|<=2 and exhibits a nonzero Jacobi
combination. The original finite summation-by-parts Leibniz defect
is retained too. A finite Fourier ball is a valid finite diagnostic
of a displayed matrix, but is not thereby a regulator preserving
the full nonlinear diffeomorphism algebra. No ghost cancellation,
Ward identity or anomaly claim is obtained merely by this projection.
