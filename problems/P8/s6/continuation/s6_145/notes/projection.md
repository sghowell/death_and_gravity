# On-shell nonlocal remainder and the local-reference boundary

The summed two-point function is an even O(4) scalar. In the uniform
soft expansion, odd degrees vanish, degree zero is a mass constant,
and degree two is proportional to p^2. These local terms may first
be regulated and subtracted; they are not assumed to be finite
as separate unpaired momentum integrals.

Keep the tail of soft degree at least four before loop integration.
The regional majorants are integrable and uniform on the closed
outer disc |s-1|<=2. They justify exchanging the convergent projected
series and integrations. Evenness provides descent through the
square-root coordinate s=0, and continuation from the common
Euclidean germ yields a holomorphic invariant on a neighborhood
of the disc. This is the analyticity of the named finite perturbative
increment, not a whole-theory high-energy contour theorem.

Let its supremum be E and expand in s-1 on radius two. Each coefficient
a_n is bounded by E/2^n. The on-shell affine operation kills n=0,1.
For |s-1|<=1/2, therefore,

 |Pi(s)-Pi(1)-(s-1)Pi'(1)|/|s-1|^2
 <= E/[4(1-(1/2)/2)] = E/3.

The value at s=1 is defined by the removable limit. The affine map
also kills any arbitrary mass and kinetic polynomial, even with
regulator poles or finite dimensional coefficients.

Crucially this does NOT determine Pi_MS(1), Pi_MS'(1), the finite
second-order field normalization or the common-MS mass reference.
The bound cannot alone establish the canonical two-loop pole.
Those missing terms are explicitly retained in the frontier.
