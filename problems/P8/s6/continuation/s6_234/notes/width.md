# First cuts, identical rate and the outgoing sign

The equal-light-mass heavy bubble is g² B0(s;1,1)/(32pi²), up to real local counterterms. For s>4, its Feynman denominator is negative between x_-=(1-beta)/2 and x_+=(1+beta)/2, beta=sqrt(1-4/s). On this interval Log(Q-i0)=log(abs(Q))-i pi. The minus logarithm therefore gives

Im Pi_H,first(s+i0)=g² beta/(32pi)>0.

The interval length and endpoints are verified independently. Real polynomial subtractions do not change this cut. The factor1/2 is the identical internal-line symmetry factor; using the distinct heavy-light factor here doubles the result incorrectly.

For the mixed light bubble the two roots instead have separation

sqrt[(s-(M_H+1)²)(s-(M_H-1)²)]/s.

Above its physical threshold, Im Pi_phi,first is g²/(16pi) times this separation, without the identical factor. This statement is only for the first bubble, not the complete model's quantum thresholds.

The fully integrated two-body phase space at the heavy mass is beta/(8pi). Combining flux1/(2M_H), exactly one identical final1/2!, and the literal decay vertex g gives

Gamma_H,first=g² beta/(32pi M_H)
            =Im Pi_H,first(M_H²)/M_H.

At the actual parameters beta>999/1000 and3<pi<22/7. Rational bounds give

10^-208<7*999*g²/(1000*32*22*M_H²)
       <Gamma_H,first/M_H
       <g²/(96M_H²)<10^-207.

The inverse sign is s-M_H²+Pi_H. Continuing the upper boundary to the outgoing second sheet gives the formal first resonance displacement with imaginary part -Im Pi_H,first(M_H²), hence decay rather than growth. This is not an off-real first-sheet pole statement.

The leading coefficient does not determine an exact physical width or a controlled resonance approximation. Near the pole, ordinary fixed-order expansions are not uniform. An unstable heavy state cannot be replaced by an exact stable delta atom merely because its first width-to-mass ratio is small. No all-order spectral or common-parent conclusion follows.
