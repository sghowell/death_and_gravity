# Complete proper forest and exact finite MS coefficients

The frozen proper UV counterterms are, with their pole 1/epsilon,

 z_psi=-(Y/2+a C_F)/Q,
 eta_total=nu_total=(Y-4a C_F)/Q,

where eta is the relative total fermion mass insertion and nu the
relative total scalar Yukawa insertion. They are not the canonical
bare Yukawa map, which would include the separate Phi field factor.

A first fermion bubble varies under the two fermion propagator
insertions and the two Yukawa insertions as

 delta Pi_1=2(nu-z)Pi_1+(eta-z)m d_m Pi_1.

This follows independently by expanding the constant fermion-field
rescaling and mass ratio. The one-loop Euclidean p^2 coefficient is

 (2NY/Q)exp(gamma_E epsilon)(mu/m)^(2epsilon)
 Gamma(epsilon)(1-2epsilon/3).

Take its mass derivative at fixed mu, giving -2epsilon times that
coefficient, before setting mu=m. Thus the multiplier is
3(1-epsilon)(Y-2a C_F)/(Q epsilon).

The whole four-fermion proper-cycle counterterm, after the chord
closes, is local mass only. Its p^2 coefficient is zero. These
mass/kinetic/Yukawa counterterms are therefore the complete proper
forest for the slope. The overlapping subgraphs are not multiplied.

Put A_e=exp(gamma_E epsilon)Gamma(1+epsilon). The scalar paired
slope, in units NY^2/Q^2, is

 [A_e^2 R_s(epsilon)
  +6 A_e(1-epsilon)(1-2epsilon/3)]/epsilon^2,

 R_s=2(2epsilon-3)(6epsilon^3-14epsilon^2-4epsilon+3)
     /[3(epsilon-2)(epsilon-1)(2epsilon+1)].

The gauge paired slope, in units NYa C_F/Q^2, is

 [A_e^2 R_g(epsilon)
  -12 A_e(1-epsilon)(1-2epsilon/3)]/epsilon^2,

 R_g=4(2epsilon-3)(6epsilon^3-14epsilon^2-epsilon+3)
     /[3(epsilon-2)(2epsilon+1)].

The exact Laurent coefficients are respectively

 scalar: 3/epsilon^2-5/(2epsilon)+49/12,
 gauge: -6/epsilon^2+5/epsilon-37/6.

The remaining overall MS kinetic counterterm removes the poles,
not these finite terms. The pi^2 constants cancel between the raw
two-loop Gamma factor and the proper one-loop pole insertion.
This cancellation uses their distinct dimensional factors; setting
epsilon to zero in a numerator first gives an incorrect finite result.

These are Euclidean p^2 slopes. The continued Minkowski s slope
has the opposite sign. Scalar exchange here is only a massless
reference; its actual mass-one correction is retained next.
