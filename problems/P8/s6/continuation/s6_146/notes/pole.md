# Actual zero-soft and on-shell primitive slope enclosures

Take the sum of the absolute massless scalar and gauge finite
references, in the same interaction scheme:

 Z0 <=N/Q_lo^2 [49Y_hi^2/12+37Y_hi a_hi C_F/6].

Add the complete scalar mass correction from the previous proof.
No absolute value is applied to an inferred amplitude sign;
both sectors are bounded by triangle inequalities.

S6.145 bounds the same actual primitive soft tail T(s) by E
on |s-1|<=2. It is the invariant Taylor tail about s=0 starting
at s^2, hence T'(0)=0. A Cauchy derivative bound at the center
s=1 gives |T'(1)|<=E/2. Therefore the on-shell MS primitive slope
differs from its zero-momentum MS slope by at most E/2.

This is a different use of the prior disc from its divided
on-shell remainder bound E/3. Both disc radii and the missing
linear Taylor coefficient are explicit; the finite MS slope
is not inferred just from an arbitrary affine-subtracted function.

Their sum remains below 1e-410. The Euclidean and Minkowski
slopes have opposite signs, with the same absolute bound.
The exact massless Minkowski coefficient along Y=(19/45)a
has factor 3509/540 in NYa/Q^2, but no full matched amplitude

This bounds only the two quadratic primitive slopes and their
previously established nonlocal remainders. It does not evaluate
the finite MS mass references, other parameter/counterterm
insertions, the canonical field-factor products, or the full
two-loop pole. Those contributions remain separately owned.
