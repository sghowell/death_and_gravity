# Pointwise quantitative regulator bound

Let Q(t),A,B be the canonical complete-tree sew of the polarization
proof. Put a>=||A||_F,b>=||B||_F. Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive
D4 TT form gives |<A,B>_4|<=ab. Also |tr A|<=sqrt(2)a and
|tr B|<=sqrt(2)b. Each coefficient in the exact cubic is therefore
bounded, without discarding interference:

|Q(t)-Q(0)|<=t(a^2+2ab)+t^2(b^2+2ab)+t^3 b^2.

For0<=epsilon<=1/8, t=epsilon/(1+epsilon)<=epsilon and t<=1/9.
Hence

|Q(epsilon)-Q(0)|
 <=epsilon[a^2+(20/9)ab+(10/81)b^2].

All constants are exact; the same bound holds when a,b overestimate
the norms. The first epsilon derivative at zero is
2Re<A,B>_4+|tr A|^2/2, because dt/depsilon=1 at zero.

This is pointwise away from scalar-soft and internal propagator poles.
Although Q is nonnegative for real D>=4, this alone constructs neither
a noninteger-dimensional Hilbert space nor an integrable real-minus-soft
bound. The soft 1/omega behavior still requires a matched subtraction.
Neither the old matter-only S296 integrated result nor S304's D4
gravity bound is automatically an integrated full-D gravity theorem.
Finite virtual hard matching is a further independent obligation.
