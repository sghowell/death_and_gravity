# Regulated source square and the reducible vacuum graph

The stationary first H source is fixed, not adjustable:

    J1,D=-G T_D(1)/2.

Exact Gaussian elimination of H gives
-(hJ1,D+G Phi^2/2)^2/(2M). At order h^2 the connected
vacuum functional therefore contains

    heavy-reducible double bubble: -g T_D(1)^2/(8M),
    source mass insertion:        -G J1,D T_D(1)/(2M),
    source square:                -J1,D^2/(2M).

The coefficients are respectively -1/8,+1/4,-1/8 times
g T_D(1)^2/M. They cancel before any finite-part operation.
This proves equivalence to evaluating the full-field 1PI
potential at stationary H=0, where the reducible graph is
absent. Omitting just the source square or adding it on top
of a full-field 1PI potential is incorrect.

The cancellation requires full regulated J1,D. In Q units,
with ell=log(mF^2),

    T_D(1)=-1/epsilon-(ell+1)
      -epsilon[1+ell+ell^2/2+pi^2/12]+O(epsilon^2).

Consequently the finite source-square contribution is

    -g[2ell^2+4ell+3+pi^2/6]/(8M Q^2),

not -g(ell+1)^2/(8M Q^2), the square of the finite
source alone. Both pole-times-epsilon products are present.
The other two members of the triple contain the matching
products, so the entire finite triple is zero.

For J=hJ1+h^2J2, its square has coefficients h^2J1^2,
2h^3J1J2 and h^4J2^2. J2 has no vacuum contribution at
order two: its induced Phi2 term needs another loop.
The complete J2 reference remains a separate open
calculation, including the already bounded S6.148 sector.
