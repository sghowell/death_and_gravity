# Both scalar-force covariance conventions

For the full unintegrated source sector at fixed physical
CD metric and the same canonical conditional measure,

    (F_eta-<F_eta>)/kappa
       =-m/sqrt(kappa) integral a^3 A_centered.DS_eta.

The scalar retuning and source contact are deterministic.
Use the same-state CD covariance bound with
||DS_eta||<=2048delta U_eta and
||grad DS_eta||<=400000000delta U_eta.
Since T<=1, the normalized variance is at most

    delta^2/kappa [
       108m*2048^2+144*(400000000)^2/m ] ||eta||H3^2.

At m=1000 and kappa=10^800 the coefficient is below
4e-784 delta^2, so the normalized standard deviation
is below2e-392 delta ||eta||H3, and below2e-394
on the full delta<=1/100 ball.

At psi=0 the exact first source derivative is zero,
so the source-induced scalar-force operator and this
noise vanish identically. Metric stress-tensor noise
does not vanish by this argument. For homogeneous
source histories the full causal mean force still
vanishes; that separate fact is not a covariance
bound for arbitrary inhomogeneous test directions.

This completes only a conditional smeared scalar-force
observable. It is not a stochastic metric/light solution,
small full inverse or corrected nonlinear quantum bounce.

## Canonical normalization cannot be skipped

Let eta_Phi=sqrt(kappa)eta_u and
Phi_pert=sqrt(kappa)psi. The unnormalized canonical
force pairing is F_Phi[eta_Phi]=F_u[eta_Phi/sqrt(kappa)].
Its variance is therefore bounded by

    delta^2 [108m*2048^2+144*(400000000)^2/m]
      ||eta_Phi||H3^2,

with NO remaining kappa suppression. Its standard
deviation is below2e8 delta ||eta_Phi||H3.
The much smaller normalized display must not be
presented as this canonical bound.

On the explicit smaller ball delta<=1e-14, the
canonical standard deviation is below2e-6 per unit
test norm. The same previous full-source mean-force
bound transforms to4e10 delta^2 times the canonical
perturbation/test norm product, hence below4e-18
times that product on this smaller ball.
These are scoped smeared source-sector controls;
they do not control the full scalar/metric solution.
