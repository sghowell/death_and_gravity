# What one basis direction does and does not determine

In the stated local six-derivative truncation, the complete remaining
physical-TT polynomial must equal chi*T. The coefficient extraction API
first requires an exact entire polynomial in the constrained a_i,H_ij
variables, computes its candidate coefficient, and verifies equality of
the WHOLE polynomial. It rejects floats, unknown extra symbols,
nonpolynomial/rational kernels, nonreal coefficients and any out-of-span
remainder. Zero is accepted only as a supplied complete zero polynomial,
never chosen as a default physical coefficient.

The real local Hermitian coefficient is understood with the common
amplitude convention stripped. Rational, square-root and logarithmic
coefficient recoveries are diagnostics of the map, not substitutions for
the original source. A non-Bose word and a higher-derivative Bose-symmetric
polynomial are negative controls. The function is not a projection which
silently erases inconvenient terms.

A basis theorem specifies how much matching data is needed WITHIN ITS
TRUNCATION, not the data's value. Zero additional selected-matter UV pole
does not select a finite subtraction. The previously retained flat
one-loop information cannot see the loop-counted chi direction.
An actual physical parent matching calculation, not a naturalness guess
or a user's arbitrary choice, must determine it or provide a sufficient
bound.

One amplitude value cannot isolate chi in the presence of unknown
higher-derivative or nonlocal contributions. Nor does the known selected
minimal-matter remainder bound1e-196 bound a sum with an unassigned
curvature term. Internal-graviton loops and full virtual/inclusive
matching remain separate obligations.
