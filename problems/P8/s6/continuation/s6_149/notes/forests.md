# Overlapping proper subgraphs, Ward identity and flavor ownership

Each vacuum theta graph has two vertices and three labeled edges:
fermion_1, fermion_2 and boson. Its three proper cycles are all
two-edge subsets. Every pair overlaps; there is no disjoint
product forest. The whole fermion cycle has two external boson
legs; the other two cycles are proper fermion self energies.

In the scalar row the whole-cycle mass/residue reference is
paired in f_R,D, as stated in scalar.md. Its counterterm
occurrence is therefore removed from the separate
(1/2)Tr[D_B(S_B^(1))''] insertion ledger. The two fermion
self-energy counterterms combine into
-Tr[D_F^(-1)D_F^(1)] for this coupling class. This occurrence
is also not included again as a separate matching insertion.

In the gauge row, the vector Ward identity follows from

    S_0(D_1-D_0)S_1=S_0-S_1,

where D_1-D_0 is the contracted gauge vertex. Closing the
fermion loop makes the contracted polarization a difference of
translated integrals. Dimensional regularization preserves
that translation and the vector current; no gamma-five trace is
involved. The UV counterterm is transverse and proportional to
q^2 delta_mu_nu-q_mu q_nu, not a vector mass. Contracting with
the massless gauge covariance gives a multiple of Integral_q 1,
which is scaleless. The fermion mass/kinetic counterterms do
not vanish and remain exactly those in massless.md.

The scalar Yukawa loop includes the two opposite-sign active
flavors and three colors, N_s=6. A vacuum with no external
Phi insertions also receives gauge exchange from all twelve
inert flavors. Thus N_g=14*3=42, not six. Its finite contribution
is 18N_g aC_Fm^4/Q^2 with C_F=4/3. The flavor ledger enumerates
all 42 states; odd-Yukawa cancellation is not used to delete
inert gauge states.

The remaining overall subtraction is a vacuum constant.
Its finite part is not assigned zero. Other pure-scalar and
matching contributions and the order-one H-source square
belong to the eventual complete vacuum-energy reference.
The source expansion gives -J1^2/(2M) at order two,
-J1 J2/M at order three and -J2^2/(2M) at order four.
No full-reference sum is claimed here.
