# Whole Ward identity and the zero-transfer boundary

Let K(p^2) be the full regulated inverse scalar propagator, with the
stripped metric vertex convention of notes/source. Diffeomorphism
invariance of a background-covariant effective action gives

q_mu Gamma^{mu nu}=p^nu K(pprime^2)-pprime^nu K(p^2), q=pprime-p.

For a continuous vertex and differentiable K at the point in question,
expand this identity for arbitrary infinitesimal q. It gives
Gamma0=2p^mu p^nu Kprime(p^2)-eta^{mu nu}K(p^2).
An independent whole constant-metric variation derives the same result:
vary sqrt(-g)K(g^mn p_m p_n) with covariant Fourier momenta fixed.
The volume term and inverse-metric term are both retained, in all ten
independent symmetric metric directions.

For a full background loop this covariance argument includes the loop
measure and every scalar/graviton index contraction. Background metric
dependence of the gauge-fixing and ghost operators is also included.
It is not valid to vary only one scalar propagator. The background
identity is not automatically the ordinary quantum-h proper vertex.

At a regulated simple pole K(mu)=0 and Z=1/Kprime(mu), Z Gamma0=2pp.
For K=p^2-mu+Sigma, the two endpoint vertices give+2Sigma_prime at
one loop and the four external scalar LSZ factors give-2Sigma_prime.
The entire S283 raw expression cancels, including finite7, gamma and
log4pi. Its UV4mu/eps and IR-mu/eps origins remain distinct. Neither the
identity nor this cancellation proves an exact physical one-particle
pole, infrared continuity or a dressed/detector observable.

In particular, the analytic symmetric transverse tensor
Q=t PP-(P.q)(Pq+qP)+(P.q)^2 eta obeys q.Q=0.
Adding xi Q changes the on-shell F1 slope by xi/2 while preserving
K and F1(0). It can arise from a Ricci-derivative curvature operator.
Thus the numerical slope relation in the next note is not a universal
Ward consequence. Singular transverse structures can also invalidate an
assumed massless zero-transfer limit.

The auxiliary accepted pair kernel M(t)=int_0^1dx/[4mu-t(1-x^2)] has
R(t)=V(t)M(t)-mu/2=-11t/12+t^2/(10mu)+t^3/(84mu^2)+...
For0<=tau<=mu its exact positive integrand is
tau[tau+mu(7+x^2)/2]/[4mu+tau(1-x^2)].
Bounding the denominator gives11tau/12<=R(-tau)<=7tau/6.
This proves only that pair-kernel limit, not a whole physical 1PI IR
factor or detector theorem.
