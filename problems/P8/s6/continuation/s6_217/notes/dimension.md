# Both analytic branches at the same dimension

At real dimension the physical coefficient is the positive imaginary part of the sharp-detector/annihilation-source product \(F_+\). Introduce the opposite algebraic branch \(F_\sharp\), obtained by reversing every explicit phase/amplitude \(i\) at real parameters. The analytic continuation is
\[
 (F_+(d)-F_\sharp(d))/(2i),
\]
with both functions evaluated at the same \(d\). It is not \(\operatorname{Im}F_+(d)\) for complex \(d\), which is generally not holomorphic.

On the real unit clock interval the bare frequencies are real. The sum of the two absolute phase defects is less than \(1/20\). Hence either sign of the phase exponential has modulus at most
\[
 e^{1/20}<\frac1{1-1/20}=\frac{20}{19}<2.
\]
The geometric-series inequality follows termwise from \(1/n!\le1\), with strictness beyond degree1. No unit-modulus assertion is made at complex dimension.

For the UV-subtracted endpoints the exact sign changes preserve S213's full frequency-domain and active/passive angular majorants. The identities hold on the real dimensional interval and extend analytically; no dimension parameter is conjugated. The same angular exponent \(-1/8\), far radial exponent \(-7/4\), and near transfer degree \(21/4<6\) give an integrable all-transfer majorant. Dominated convergence therefore identifies the corrected endpoint complement with its physical-dimensional integral. The proof is not limited to a fixed external momentum.

The original renormalization prescription leaves the already absolutely convergent actual-state correction and finite time remainder in physical dimension. The explicit two-phase estimate additionally rules out a new exponential obstruction if the reference pair is continued. It does not define a physical noninteger-dimensional Gaussian state.
