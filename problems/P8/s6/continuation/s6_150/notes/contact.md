# The same fixed contact and its dimensional lift

Use Q=16 pi^2, M>1 and fixed g. S6.125's convergent potential
contact is

    sigma=6[(-L+g/M)g J1+g^2 J2]/Q = -k L+c,
    k=6g J1/Q, c=6g^2(J1/M+J2)/Q,
    J1=integral_0^infinity y/[(y+1)^2(y+M)] dy,
    J2=integral_0^infinity y/[(y+1)^2(y+M)^2] dy.

The exact anchored primitives are imported and checked at
epsilon zero; both J's are strictly positive. A convenient
common dimensional lift replaces y by y^(1-epsilon) in the
measure and multiplies by exp(gamma epsilon) mu^(2 epsilon)/
Gamma(2-epsilon). It remains affine in L, with coefficients
k_D,c_D. At the origin its absolute radial behavior is
y^(1-Re epsilon), and the slowest large-radius term is
y^(-2-Re epsilon). It is holomorphic in -1<Re epsilon<2.
On compact substrips any number of parameter/epsilon derivatives
has an integrable bound with powers of log y. In particular the
same lift is finite near zero even though later I0 products have
poles.

Nothing below depends on choosing this particular evanescent
extension: the algebra holds for any COMMON analytic lift of the
same contact. Use the inherited lift in both insertion and
coordinate/counterterm conversion. Replacing it by sigma_0 in
only one pole-containing leg is not allowed. No four-dimensional
condition alone is claimed to fix arbitrary evanescent terms.

With A=(L-g/M)/2 and F=A-g/(y+M), the integrand is
6y(F^2-A^2)/[Q(y+1)^2]. At the actual L>3g/M, both F and A are
positive and F<A. Thus sigma<0 pointwise after integration.
It is a fixed signed contact, not an adjustable error allowance.
