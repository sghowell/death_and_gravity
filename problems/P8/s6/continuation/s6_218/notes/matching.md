# Full dimensional action and varying volume

For an arbitrary positive isotropic scale \(A(t)\) in spatial dimension \(d\),
\[
 K_T=A^{2-d},\quad K_L=A^{2-d}\omega^2/m^2,\quad
 \omega^2=m^2+k^2/A^2.
\]
There are \(d-1\) transverse and one longitudinal modes. Put \(H=A'/A\), \(p=-zH\), \(z=k^2/(A^2\omega^2)\). Direct differentiation of these complete scalar Hamiltonians gives
\[
 s_T=((d-2)H+p)/2,\quad s_L=((d-2)H-p)/2,
 \qquad t_s=s_s'-p s_s.
\]
The generic S193 variational identity for \(KV=\omega^2I\) identifies the actual fourth-order current with the variation of
\[
 L_0=-d\omega/2,\quad L_2=\frac{(d-1)s_T^2+s_L^2}{4\omega},\quad
 L_4=\frac{(d-1)(t_T^2+s_T^4)+t_L^2+s_L^4}{16\omega^3}.
\]
These are finite local marker orders, not a convergent all-order determinant expansion or a new state.

At each fixed time, evaluating the local radial integral with \(k=Aq\) gives the full \(A^d\) Jacobian. One evaluates this density first and then varies it; there is no time-dependent momentum relabeling in the actual mode ODE. For the normalized \(z^n\) integrals the ratio is \((d/2)_n/(\alpha)_n\), with \(\alpha=1/2\) for second order and \(3/2\) for fourth order.

In the original \(-+++\) curvature convention,
\[
 R=d[2H'+(d+1)H^2],\quad
 Ric^2=d^2(H'+H^2)^2+d(H'+dH^2)^2,
\]
\[
 Riem^2=4d(H'+H^2)^2+2d(d-1)H^4,
\quad a_{4s}=(5R^2-2Ric^2+2Riem^2)/360,
\]
\[
 a_{4V}=d\,a_{4s}+Ric^2/2-R^2/6-Riem^2/12.
\]
The exact normalized second-order radial polynomial equals
\[
 (d-6)R/24-d(d-6)(H'+dH^2)/12.
\]
The fourth-order polynomial, in the normalization before the common \(1/16\) and radial factor, equals
\[
 2a_{4V}+c_4(d)\,[dH^4+3H^2H'],\qquad
 c_4(d)=-4d(5d^3-96d^2+229d-126)/945.
\]
The differences are weighted total time derivatives of \(A^dH\) and \(A^dH^3\). Their compact variations vanish at general dimension, before the dimension limit. The scalar Hamiltonian differentiation and a separate rational radial-moment derivation independently reproduce both polynomials.

Continue \(d=3-2\epsilon_{\rm DR}\) with the original radial normalization. The vacuum, Einstein and curvature gamma factors are the same as S193. Subtract the original four-dimensional scalar pole coefficients while keeping the same continued metric volume and invariant contractions on both sides. In particular the volume is \(A^d\), not \(a^d e^\phi\) at noninteger \(d\). Normalize the physical trace only after taking the finite limit.

At \(\mu=m\), the physical finite local density is
\[
 \frac{A^3}{64\pi^2}
 \left[\frac52m^4+\frac53m^2R-\frac{R^2}{30}-\frac{Ric^2}{15}\right],
\]
modulo the compact four-dimensional Euler variation. This uses
\(-4a_{4s}=-R^2/30-Ric^2/15-Euler/45\) only after the dimension limit. The evanescent vacuum coefficient is \(5/2\), not \(9/2\), and the Einstein coefficient is \(5/3\), not1. The cosmological term has nonzero trace variation. No new finite coefficient or alternative state has been selected.
