# Finite complement, entire tail and the actual-parent boundary

The preceding note supplies the same numerical upper bounds for every mixed frequency, rotation, squeeze and complete physical current vertex used in S190–S192. Their recurrences and comparison estimates are generic matrix statements; their transfer here uses the newly checked hypotheses.

The finite tenth-order Riccati reference and its parameter derivatives are bounded by the old positive majorant recurrences. The reference graph stays below \(1/100\), with the original nonzero initial mismatch retained. The actual/reference high-band graph errors keep the displays
\[
 10^{30}\nu_-^{-10},\quad2\,10^{32}\nu_-^{-9},\quad

\]
The complete real covariance map includes both adjoint directions. Its first three Fréchet bounds and all iterated first-response terms therefore give the same covariance and frequency-weighted tail bounds. The infinite radial integrals are retained; their common integrable majorants justify twice differentiating into \(C^0(I)\).

In the finite complement, the actual balanced generator has symmetric part bounded by the old squeeze display11. Its fast \(\omega\) part is skew. The exact covariance IVP, its two tangent equations and the unchanged initial covariance bound give the same finite-band estimates. These are not ultraviolet estimates by themselves; the high-band argument supplies the missing tail.

The finite-reference covariance is holomorphic on the same marker contour \(|\zeta|=\omega/m\), with both algebraic sharp branches evaluated at the same marker. Its physical current is even in \(\zeta\), because the trace vertex is real block diagonal. Odd off-diagonal covariance blocks are not set to zero. Cauchy's estimate controls the full reference-minus-fourth-order current, with the old contour displays \((12,180,6000)\omega\).

Combining the actual-minus-reference part, finite complement, fourth-order subtraction and complete infinite reference tail gives
\[
 \|\partial_\epsilon^b(J_{\rm actual}-J_{\rm ad4})\|_{C^0(I)}
 <(10^{77},10^{94},10^{111})_b,\qquad b=0,1,2.
\]
This is a coordinate-density current. No claim that the varying \(A^3\) is amplitude independent is used. The fixed local matching in the next note is needed before this comparison becomes the prescribed Gaussian determinant current.

There is an important parent boundary. S176 proves that the original affine source and its first metric variation vanish at the reference, and that the zero-mean second source insertion vanishes. Thus the actual reference metric Hessian is the connected Proca Gaussian response. Away from the reference that source need not vanish. Its Gaussian mean contribution, quadratic in the source, starts at fourth metric order when the source starts at second order. It has no reference metric Hessian, but it must not be deleted in a finite-amplitude nonlinear parent problem.

Accordingly, the uniform \(C^2\) family and its Taylor estimate here concern the Gaussian determinant component. They justify differentiating that component to obtain the actual reference response. They are not a finite-amplitude remainder bound for the full affine sourced parent, a finite-coupling background or nonlinear stability theorem.
