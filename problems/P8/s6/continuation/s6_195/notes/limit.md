# High external transfer and the ultraviolet boundary

A sharp field-space projection is a computational regulator,
not a physical ultraviolet theory or a covariant stress
subtraction. Its finite current need not separately satisfy
the covariant Ward identities. No new physical Hadamard
state is substituted: the projected calculation uses the
restriction of the actual common state's correlations.

If |p|>2K, no pair k,q=k-p lies inside the common field band.
The propagated covariance contribution is then zero. But
the second metric variation contains the pointwise product
D_-p Gamma_p, whose total momentum is zero. Its contact
need not vanish. Projecting the first vertex and then
squaring it would incorrectly discard this term.

An independent example keeps only the constant vector
oscillator and uses gamma=epsilon D cos(p.x), tr D=0.
The mean first exponential derivative is zero, but the mean
second derivative is D^2/2. At a=1 and mass m, with physical
vacuum covariance diag(I/(2m),m I/2), the contact current
derivative is exactly -m tr(D^2)/4, nonzero for D!=0.
It comes from the full exponential before projection.

Conversely, a finite-band bound uniform in external p does
NOT control the internal ultraviolet limit. The memory
majorant grows as nu_k nu_q; at fixed p its radial integral
has positive fifth-power divergence. The contact's absolute
majorant also grows. This is not evidence that the properly
renormalized full physical response diverges: cancellations
and local covariant subtraction have not yet been analyzed
for this full spatial kernel.

S194 fixes the complete homogeneous subtraction/current.
The spatially varying response requires its own quantitative
infinite-tail and fixed-prescription argument. No cutoff,
order reduction, pole deletion, full inverse or stability
verdict is inferred from the projected calculation.
