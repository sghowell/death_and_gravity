# From a Cauchy tail to a finite integrated action allowance

On the unit canonical jet cube, contraction counting gives
|Y|<=4, |L3|,|L4|<=64 and |L5|<=256. Exact weighted
coefficient majorants of the sixth/eighth densities give

    E6=2.858465025e-1594,
    0<E8<3e-2195.

The full density as a function of common scalar amplitude t
is even and bounded by M=10^811 on |t|=rho=10^300.
Cauchy's coefficient estimate bounds the absolute sum of
all powers starting at degree ten, evaluated at |t|<=1, by

    E10=M/[rho^10(1-rho^-2)] <2e-2189.

Thus every unretained field degree is assigned: six, eight
and all even degrees at least ten. This does not bound
quantum loops or unbounded loop-momentum integration.

At a physical point define W as the maximum absolute value
of Phi and its four first and sixteen ordered second jets.
For W>0 divide those jets by W and use the uniform cube
estimate; if W=0 the remainder is zero. This gives

    |L_full-L2-L4|<=E6 W^6+E8 W^8+E10 W^10
                   <=(E6+E8+E10)W^2  for W<=1.

Let Psi be real Schwartz, with Fourier support in the
Euclidean four-momentum unit ball and Fourier L1 norm at
most one in the S6.111 convention. Every jet has L-infinity
norm at most one and L2 norm at most U=||Psi||_2.
The maximum is bounded by the sum of the 21 squared jets, so

    integral W^2 <=21 U^2,
    |S_full_target-S_L2_L4| <=21(E6+E8+E10)U^2
                           <1e-1591 U^2.

The integrated coefficient is approximately
6.0027765525e-1593. A pointwise constant bound over infinite
spacetime would not imply this result; the W rescaling and
Parseval step are essential. The analytic density starts at
degree two, so its action on this class is integrable.
