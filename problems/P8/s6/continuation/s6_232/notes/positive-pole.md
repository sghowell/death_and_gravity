# Separately named positive rational pole diagnostic

This is a mathematical control for the positive dispersion data, not a change to the original parent, state or prescription. Let

D=2lambda/gamma,
M_H²=D+2,
g²=gamma D^4=1/2^26.

The last equality uses the actual original lambda and gamma. The pole lies above the scalar threshold4 and below the named split10^198. Define the crossing-symmetric rational amplitude

A_atom(s,t,u)=g²[1/(M_H²-s)+1/(M_H²-t)+1/(M_H²-u)].

Its s-channel discontinuity as a positive spectral measure is rho_atom=pi*g² delta(S-M_H²). This unknown heavy pole is retained, not subtracted as a known original light pole. The t-channel term is independent of v and contributes only to the subtraction function in the present forward-coefficient calculation.

Direct differentiation gives

b2_atom(t)=2g²/(D+t/2)^3,
b20_atom=4lambda,
b21_atom=-3gamma,
b40_atom=2g²/D^5=gamma²/lambda,
J4_atom=2g²/D^4=2gamma.

The atom supplies the required low spectral weight and saturates b20*b40-J4²=0. Adding a second positive support point makes the Gram determinant strictly positive. Thus the derived moment inequalities are compatible with these two original low coefficients at the level of positive measures. The mandatory higher coefficient is nonzero, in contrast to the original tree.

Only b20 and b21 are matched. The full rational amplitude, its constant potential term, its higher coefficients and its full-field dependence are not identified with the original S177 tree or function family. No new light/heavy action or matching counterterm is installed.

In particular this real rational tree is NOT an exact unitary scalar S matrix. Away from its pole it has a nonzero real scattering amplitude but lacks the light elastic absorptive cut required by the optical theorem. Its delta is a positive-measure diagnostic, not a controlled physical width or narrow-resonance approximation. Saturation of a positive-measure bound does not establish the optimum attainable by an exact interacting S matrix.

There is no demonstrated full quantum UV completion, finite-gravity matching or common-bounce parent here. The purpose is to prevent the false inference that a required enhancement over the first elastic contribution excludes every possible additional channel.
