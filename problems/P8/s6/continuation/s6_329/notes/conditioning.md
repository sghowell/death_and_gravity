# Exact total-energy events and normalized infrared limit

Finite cutoff observations use the event R_eta<=x, not the limiting
event by fiat and not separate bounds on individual emitted energies.
Because R_eta increases to R, these events decrease exactly to R<=x.
For R>x, the partial energy eventually exceeds x; for R<=x, it never
does. This includes the equality case R=x without needing to assume
absence of an atom. Independently, the S299 positive-x CDF has no atom
at x for a>0; at a=0 all mass is at R=0.

Define the marked field to be zero when its cutoff event fails.
Inside the event its norm is bounded by293<300. On R<=x it converges
uniformly in the angular TT norm by S328; on R>x the indicators
eventually vanish. Hence the zero-extended marked fields converge
almost surely and in every finite Lp by dominated convergence.
In particular, a bound on a single polarization is not substituted
for the uniform tensor-field norm.

The event probabilities converge to F(a)*x^a>0 for a>0 and to1
for a=0. Dividing the converging marked expectations by these actual
probabilities gives the conditioned limit. Bounded continuous marked
moments have the same limit; powers of the coefficient difference
are bounded on the event and are included.

The factor F(a)*x^a is kept unexpanded, including at extremely small
resolution. Remove eta at fixed positive x and fixed kappa first.
A later correlated x(kappa) limit is a separate operation. This
does not assert a simultaneous limit for a varying cutoff event.
