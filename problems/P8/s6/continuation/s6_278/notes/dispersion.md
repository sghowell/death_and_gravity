# What the refreshed gravitational literature does and does not supply

The new observable route uses a finite physical detector resolution,
distinct from a dimensional infrared regulator. In the analytic soft
prescription, remove the latter after stripping, while retaining the
former. Do not identify a classical kappa limit with the detector scaling
limit. The original minimal pole coefficient is -1/(kappa t). For the
illustrative weight q and t=-q^2 its integral is log(Q/E)/kappa:
fixed log(Q/E) gives zero as kappa grows; holding log(Q/E)/kappa fixed
gives a nonzero limit. No positivity of this illustrative UV functional
is assumed.

The finite-resolution bounds of
[Bellazzini et al.,2512.13780v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13780v2),
section5, retain finite-coupling errors. Their numerical gravity example
also has a different spectrum. Applying this route here requires the
massive channel kernels, admissibility for every UV spin and energy,
all low cuts, full finite matching and a bound on the detector error.
A parametric big-O term is not a certified number at kappa10^800.
Their printed fixed-t angular kernel is not used: our physical convention
is cos(theta)=1-2q^2/(s-4m^2).

[Tokuda, Aoki and Hirano,2007.15009v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.15009v2),
sections3.2--3.4, keep a finite remainder after combining the graviton
pole with Regge contributions. Bounding it requires additional residue
and trajectory information.
[Caron-Huot and Tokuda,2406.07606v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07606v2),
sections2.1 and5, explain in a specific string-inspired loop calculation
why that near-forward remainder can depend strongly on light masses.
Neither source supplies that remainder for this parent.

The present result supplies actual missing IR data: the full M1 massless
cut and an explicit subtraction. It does not replace the adopted original
fixed-negative-transfer Regge contract or assert that this alternative
detector route has already met it. No numerical sign allowance is selected
by fiat, and no new user preference is needed for the remaining research.
