# Complete first normalization at nonzero regulator

Let k_D=r_D-fp_MS,D=k0+epsilon k1+..., with the same signs
as S6.133's Minkowski inverse. The scalar r_D and its first
epsilon coefficient are fixed in S6.153:

    r0=(g/Q) integral b,
    r1=(g/Q) integral b [ell-log D],
    D=xM+(1-x)^2, b=x(1-x)/D.

For the fermion use its FULL dimensional trace reduction,
not a four-dimensional formula guessed inside a pole.
Set a=x(1-x), Delta=m^2-a s. Differentiating the bubble/tadpole
trace at fixed mu gives, in units 2NY e^(gamma epsilon)
mu^(2epsilon) Gamma(epsilon)/Q, the density

    -Delta^(-epsilon)
      +epsilon(4m^2-s) a Delta^(-1-epsilon).

Its difference from -2(3-2epsilon)a Delta^(-epsilon)
is the NEGATIVE derivative of a(1-2x)Delta^(-epsilon).
Both boundary values vanish. This exact parameter IBP
establishes the reduced derivative at nonzero epsilon.

After removing the full MS kinetic pole -2NY/(Q epsilon),
the finite-regulator slope is

    fp_MS,D=-4NY/(Q epsilon) [
      e^(gamma epsilon) Gamma(1+epsilon)(3-2epsilon)
      integral a exp(epsilon log(mu^2/Delta)) -1/2 ].

Now set s=1 and mu=m. With l_x=-log(1-a/m^2),
J1=integral a l_x and J2=integral a l_x^2, its coefficients are

    fp0=4NY/Q [1/3-3J1],
    fp1=4NY/Q [2J1-3J2/2-pi^2/24].

The pi^2 term is from the SECOND coefficient of
e^(gamma epsilon) Gamma(1+epsilon) divided by epsilon.
It is not optional in the finite field/pole product.
Consequently k0=r0-fp0 and k1=r1-fp1. The new two-loop
slope is not assigned a value in this calculation.
