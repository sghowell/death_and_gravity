# One external normalization and the bounded first-field part

Let A=A0+h A1+h^2 A2 be the complete renormalized amputated
amplitude in the MS Phi field. Taking epsilon zero only
after the regulated renormalized sums gives

    Acanonical=K^(-2) A
      =A0+h(A1-2k0 A0)
       +h^2[A2-2k0 A1+(3k0^2-2t0)A0].

The genuinely new second normalization t0 remains a
separate term. The part determined by the first normalization
is exactly 3k0^2 A0-2k0 A1.

S6.133 already bounds the canonical one-loop coefficient
C1=A1-2k0 A0. Therefore, if its relative absolute bound is E
and |k0|<=k_up, the AMPUTATED coefficient obeys
|b2(A1)|/tree<=E+2k_up. The first-field second contribution
is then bounded by

    3 k_up^2+2 k_up(E+2 k_up).

A second LSZ copy on C1 would instead double count the
known first normalization. The coordinate-map commutator
is likewise not another term in the displayed physical
amplitude. It records the local allocation when minimal
poles are separated in transformed coefficients.

For the common regular field factor, L and g both scale
as K^(-2), so

    Lc-3gc/M=(L-3g/M)/K^2.

This preserves the completed-square cancellation exactly.
Separate broad intervals for L and g would manufacture
an uncertainty absent in this correlated transformation.
The coefficient-map pole commutator has a distinct fixed
local allocation; its size is recorded separately.
