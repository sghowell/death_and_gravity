# Regulated vertices and internal lines must transform together

For a constant finite field factor K_D, define
Phi_c=sqrt(K_D) Phi_MS. A vertex containing n Phi fields
has factor K_D^(-n/2); a transformed internal Phi propagator
has factor K_D. Other fields and masses are unchanged
in this isolated direction.

Every four-Phi amputated graph satisfies
sum n_vertices=2 I_Phi+4. Thus its complete field factor is

    K_D^(-(I_Phi+2)) K_D^(I_Phi)=K_D^(-2).

At first order in K_D=1+h k_D+..., a one-loop graph
therefore has the two order-two variations

    vertices: -(I_Phi+2) k_D A1_D,
    internal lines: I_Phi k_D A1_D.

Their sum is -2 k_D A1_D. Dropping the line contribution
would give a wrong result. In the reduced full-heavy
one-loop scalar family I_Phi=2: every term is quadratic
in L and g, with Phi field weight four. The complete
fermion box family has I_Phi=0 and weight two through
Y^2. Heavy propagators and all external invariants are
unchanged, so no heavy-momentum approximation is used.

In a fixed mass-one perturbative split the line factor
is realized by the correlated finite kinetic AND mass
reference proportional to the free inverse p_E^2+1.
A p_E^2-only insertion would shift the physical pole.
This is the same field change written as a local insertion,
not an additional field factor.

Assigned local counterterms and source-completed terms
transform in the same regulated functional. If
A1_raw=P/epsilon+F and A1_CT=-P/epsilon, their finite
field products separately contain -2 k1 P and +2 k1 P.
Only after adding them does the result become -2 k0 F.
This establishes the full regulated cancellation rather
than discarding epsilon terms in one selected leg.
