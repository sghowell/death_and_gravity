# Prepared inputs without a false final boundary condition

A causal classical response to a source supported inside
I generally does not vanish at t=1/2. The first formal
quantum coefficient must therefore not assume that h0
has compact support strictly inside the observation
interval.

For each smooth prepared input on a neighborhood of I,
extend it smoothly beyond the final observation time and
multiply by a smooth cutoff only farther in the future.
The original reference geometry and coefficient functions
are defined smoothly beyond I. For sufficiently small
variation amplitude the extended lapse and scale stay
positive and the same globally hyperbolic description
and initial Cauchy preparation apply. No quantitative
estimate on the future extension is used below.

The exact conditional Proca mode variation is a zero-data
inhomogeneous linear ODE whose coefficients and source
at t depend only on local input jets and the solution's
past. Two such extensions agreeing on I and the common
initial neighborhood have identical variations up to the
final observation time by ODE uniqueness. Finite
adiabatic subtractions, dimensional matching and fixed
profile terms are local and also agree there.

More quantitatively, S6.85's physical vector transport
bound uses the force-jet supremum on I, an integration
interval of length at most one, and the unmodified
reference initial mixing. Its radial majorants integrate
the full momentum range and its differentiation argument
uses these same past/local data. No future source norm
enters. Thus S6.179's selected PHYSICAL VECTOR C10-to-C0
bound continues to apply on every prefix and at the
right endpoint by continuity. The old scalar profile in
other parts of S6.85 is not imported.

Prepared zero data also imply Qstar h=0 in the initial
neighborhood: all retarded and local variations vanish
there. Consequently both h0 and h1 are valid prepared
classical inputs at their respective stages. One does
not reset the state at intermediate times or force the
computed solution back to zero at the final endpoint.
