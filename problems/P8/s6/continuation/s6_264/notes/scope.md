# Why a tiny Gaussian marginal is not the missing quantum bounce theorem

The reference Gaussian is unbounded. A very small nonzero tail of
one linear lapse observable does not give exact support inside the
nonlinear affine/lapse auxiliary branch. The spectral marginal is
well defined for each single self-adjoint band-limited field at a
fixed point and time. It is not a joint classical probability law
for noncommuting physical observables.

A bound uniform in its stated constant over separate fixed points
does not imply a probability for their simultaneous supremum.
An independent Gaussian-sequence diagnostic explicitly illustrates
this logical distinction; it is not an assertion that the actual P8
field values are independent. Infinite-volume or spacetime-supremum
claims would require additional estimates and a suitable observable
definition, not a union bound over an uncountable index set.

two-point function. It changes neither the Cauchy preparation nor
the full parent Hamiltonian. It is not a physical Wilsonian cutoff,
a torus-state replacement or a renormalized subtraction.
Covariance control on this band does not estimate omitted momenta,
all interacting loops or the H/Proca nonlocal onepoint response.
