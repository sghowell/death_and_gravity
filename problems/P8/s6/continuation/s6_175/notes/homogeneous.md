# Closed sources, homogeneous lifts and the actual CD geometry

If dS=0, choose W=S. Then F(W)=0 and W-S=0. The extra action is a sum of
terms quadratic in these two quantities. Its first variation with respect
to W, u, the matter variables and the physical metric vanishes identically
there, including integrations by parts and variations through S.
Therefore any light-target solution with a closed source has an exact
classical parent lift. This is not an on-shell substitution into the
action before variation: the complete first variation vanishes.

For any spatially homogeneous u(t), homogeneous lapse and scale factor,
the covariant source of S6.174 is S0(t)dt. Its curl is zero. Thus every
smooth homogeneous solution of that SAME full light target and M1 in
the analytic coefficient domain lifts. This goes beyond the particular
unit-clock solution. It requires vector data W=S (and its compatible
field strength); arbitrary vector excitations do not have zero stress.
For noncompact time histories it is not asserted that this data choice
equals the solution with a particular retarded boundary condition.

For the actual CD clock,

    a(t)=(1+t^2)^2,
    eta(t)=[arctan(t)+t/(1+t^2)]/2,
    d eta/dt=1/a,    eta in (-pi/4,pi/4).

The physical metric is a^2 times the flat metric on this open time slab.
Every constant-eta slice is Cauchy. To check this explicitly, parameterize
a nonconstant future causal curve by eta. Its spatial speed is at most
one. If it had an endpoint of its eta range in the interior of the slab,
its spatial coordinates would converge by the Lipschitz bound, and it
could be extended from that interior endpoint. Hence an inextendible
curve runs across the slab and meets each slice once. The usual
time orientation, smooth manifold and smooth one-form bundle are present.

This proves the global hyperbolicity hypotheses needed for the compact
source Green theorem in operator.md. The finite conformal-time interval
is not a failure of global hyperbolicity. No scattering completeness,
in/out vacuum, Hadamard covariance or interacting background response is
inferred from this causal geometry argument.
