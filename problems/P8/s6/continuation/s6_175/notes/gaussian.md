# Exact Gaussian reduction and its boundary and regulator conditions

Fix the physical metric and a common finite regulator for the vector
quadratic form and its source. Project the source consistently onto that
same regulated space. With a nonsingular boundary-prescribed operator O,
the finite-dimensional pairing identity is

    <W,OW>/2 - <W,S> + <S,S>/2
      = <W-O^-1 S,O(W-O^-1 S)>/2
        + <S,(I-O^-1)S>/2.

If the regulator does not represent the source exactly, its orthogonal
complement's contact term must also be retained. In the Euclidean
positive pairing it is nonnegative and the same total source-norm upper
bound below still applies. The displayed identity applies to a common
complete finite space, or to a limit for which the inverses and pairings
are defined. It is not a renormalized infinite determinant theorem.

Translation has unit finite-dimensional Jacobian. At fixed metric the
vector Hessian depends on g,zeta,kappa but not u, since u enters only S.
Thus the vector-only Gaussian determinant has no explicit u dependence
at fixed g. It still depends on g. In particular its stress and metric
variations cannot be dropped, even on a zero-source clock. This statement
does not fix the continuum counterterms, their finite references, the
measure for the remaining fields or their loop diagrams.

In Lorentzian signature the completion uses the specified variational
or in/out boundary inverse. It does not turn a retarded solution into
a single-branch effective action. Varying <S,G_ret S>/2 gives
(G_ret+G_ret^*)S/2, generally the retarded/advanced symmetric combination.
The adjoint relation is given in Lemma3.4.4 of the same primary Green
operator source. A causal in-in influence functional needs doubled
histories and state-dependent symmetric correlations/noise; no such
covariance or quantitative bound is supplied here.

On the clock S starts at second order in light/metric perturbations.
The source-bilinear part therefore starts at fourth order, provided
the specified inverse varies smoothly in the perturbative regulated
problem. There is no source-bilinear contribution to the classical
linearized or quadratic light equations on that clock. The determinant's
metric contributions remain distinct and need not start at fourth order.

At the Minkowski u=0 vacuum S first has field degree four; the source
bilinear first has degree eight. For a single eight-scalar vertex, the
earliest possible pure light contractions contributing to four external
scalars require two loops, to two scalars three loops, and to the vacuum
four loops. These are possible source-subgraph orders, not assertions
that each diagram is nonzero, a classification of all mixed/gravity
diagrams, or a bound on any renormalized loop integral.
