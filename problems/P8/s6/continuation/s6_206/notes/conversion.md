# Which terms the coefficient does and does not settle

For j >= 1, the S6.202 joint-domain estimate bounds the coefficient of each source time jet by a constant times nu^(1-j). At fixed P, the lost shell eventually lies entirely in that domain. Its volume is O(K^2), so the j-th endpoint conversion is O(K^(3-j)). The other four first-five endpoints are therefore at most O(K^2), and cannot cancel the leading K^3|P| conversion found from j = 0.

The complete contact has the same original one-mode band in both sides of this particular comparison. Its difference is exactly zero; its actual value is not set to zero. Its finite and divergent covariant matching remains required.

The leading difference is negative for equal nonzero real tracefree tensors. Along P = epsilon phat, it is proportional to |epsilon| with a nonzero coefficient. The opposite directional derivatives differ at zero. It is therefore not a finite spatial polynomial and cannot be silently identified with the fixed local spatial Hessian from S6.204.

This result explicitly compares two finite regulators. The one-ball side is not asserted to be renormalized or matched. The result does not authorize replacing the original cutoff, changing a physical finite counterterm, or subtracting this leading expression without deriving the rest of the conversion and matching. In particular, it does not prove a divergence of the renormalized physical current or exclusion of the CD parent.

Both external metric factors give 4/kappa. For a normalized TT tensor the canonical coefficient is 9/(64 pi^2 a kappa), still multiplying K^3|P|. At fixed finite kappa, small normalization does not control the regulator-removal limit. This is a regulator issue, not a physical cutoff or stability estimate.

The next matching calculation must retain this actual leading artifact, compute the necessary subleading artifacts and full contact/candidate-cell finite and divergent coefficients, and establish the original covariant prescription without changing its fixed finite data.
