# From the zero-momentum anchor to s=1

All quantities here are contributions of the two specified primitive
rows to the inverse scalar two-point function. No full pole is
inferred from a primitive-row estimate.

The two massless-exchange finite references have absolute upper
bounds 6.72568628844683e-13 and 1.51707209513838e-12.
Add the complete actual-scalar mass difference. Thus

    |f_MS(0)| <= 2.18964072398307e-12 approximately,
    and strictly |f_MS(0)|<10^(-10).

Use the previous zero-soft MS slope bound, without its extra
zero-to-on-shell derivative allowance. Write the exact decomposition

    f_MS(s)=f_MS(0)+s f_MS'(0)+T(s), T(0)=T'(0)=0.

S6.145 bounds T on |s-1|<=2 by E; S6.146 bounds the first
derivative at zero by the massless reference plus the actual
scalar-mass correction. Hence at s=1,

    |f_MS(1)| <= |f_MS(0)|+|f_MS'(0)|+E < 10^(-10).

The slope and tail contributions are about 2.83e-413 and 9.82e-800.
They are retained exactly despite being negligible relative to the
mass anchor. The prior on-shell slope and divided-remainder bounds
are unchanged. Together these control the local and nonlocal
pieces of these two primitive rows in the common MS interaction
scheme.

This does not insert the result into a complete canonical h^2
inverse propagator. The older W1F0 local references, fixed-source
counterterms, other matching insertions, z1^2 and z2 terms must
be combined with their actual owners before a full pole claim.
