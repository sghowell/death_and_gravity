# Countervertex ancestry and the missing curved subtraction boundary

For a connected subgraph with E_sub external legs, L_sub topological loops and countergrades r_v, replace it by its complete countervertex of degree E_sub and grade r_eff=L_sub+sum r_v. The connected identity gives
E_sub-2+2r_eff=sum_sub(d_v-2+2r_v).
Thus complete subgraph contraction preserves the weighted source filtration. Iterating over nested or disjoint subtraction forests preserves the same result. Local derivatives on external fields do not change their field count.

Lower-degree countervertices are therefore expected. A degree9 source vertex with four tadpole contractions produces a one-H countervertex at loop grade4, of weight1-2+8=7. Treating it as a tree onepoint vertex gives weight-1 and a false loop inference. Similarly a pair of degree9 source vertices with k cross-light contractions becomes degree16-2k at grade k, preserving weight14. Independent contractions check these examples and the full identity.

This statement requires that the chosen subtractions retain the full background Taylor expansion, source marker and diagram ancestry. It does not allow an independent lower-degree finite source term at an arbitrarily earlier loop order. Nor does it define a complete counterfunctional merely by subtracting an on-shell or projected jet.
