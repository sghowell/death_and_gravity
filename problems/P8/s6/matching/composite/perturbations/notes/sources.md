cross-check here. Its notation is translated as follows:

| mu/cV², equations 4.8–4.9 | finite algebraic Xi |

The paper uses -+++ while the pinned P8 convention is +---; the simultaneous
signature/action-curvature convention map leaves the displayed positive
ADM kinetic coefficients unchanged. We independently vary the actual
positive-root matrix and source action, rather than changing just an
isolated curvature sign. The paper's HR parameters are not identified with
our beta_n by their names: P and the generating elementary polynomial fix
the operational dictionary.

## Explicit 4.8 versus 4.12 normalization audit

Our own exact elimination of the stated unreduced quadratic action,
with the stated spatial-vector factor 1/2 and q=k(E-S), yields

\[
K={Ga^3\mathcal C\over16N_g},\qquad
U={N_ga^3\mu\over16}.
\]

The translated expression for C agrees with equation 4.11. Taking the
printed equation 4.12 literally in its stated same q convention instead
gives ratios to these coefficients of

\[
K_{4.12}/K=2,\qquad U_{4.12}/U={2Fy^2\over G+Fy^2}.
\]

These are not a common overall factor. Their frozen-coefficient frequency
ratio is Fy²/(G+Fy²), so they cannot both follow from the preceding
completed square with no further field transformation. We therefore use
the literal action and exact auxiliary elimination as the normalization
oracle. This is a bounded algebraic dictionary check, not a claim that all
conclusions of the source paper are invalid. The extra factors are positive
in the stipulated domain and do not reverse the relevant sign criterion.
No unspecified time-dependent redefinition is silently imported; such a
map would also need its derivative terms.

The source's scalar kinetic analysis does not provide the full rolling
scalar-gradient or finite-band matching certificate needed here. Our
tensor/vector action and bounce jets are independently checked; no scalar
health assertion is inferred from that source.

## EFT and completion cautions

The coupling construction is defined in
[de Rham, Heisenberg and Ribeiro, arXiv:1408.1678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1678).
The generic nonlinear constraint issue must be distinguished from a
low-energy EFT in
[de Rham, Heisenberg and Ribeiro, arXiv:1409.3834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3834).

[Matas, arXiv:1506.00666](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.00666), especially its
equations 1.2–1.3 and section 5.3, gives a flat-background upper-cutoff
estimate in a specified normalization. An upper bound is not a guaranteed
safe rolling-background cutoff, and it does not license arbitrary high-k
use of this gate. We have not evaluated all background-dependent canonical
scales or interactions needed to establish such a window.

[Lüben and Schmidt-May, arXiv:1804.04671](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.04671)
discusses a higher-theory completion whose leading source coupling is
composite. Its additional source-order terms and physical-metric
identification cannot be omitted when assessing that completed theory.

Accordingly the negative principal sign proved for two frozen backgrounds
is not by itself a proof of large subcutoff amplification, a quantum
front-velocity statement or a general exclusion of composite matter
coupling, bimetric theories, or ultraviolet completions.
