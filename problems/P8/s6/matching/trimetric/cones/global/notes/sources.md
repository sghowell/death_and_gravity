1. Lüben and Schmidt-May, [*Ghost-free completion of an effective matter
   coupling in bimetric theory*, arXiv:1804.04671](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.04671),
   sections 2.1–2.3 and 5.1. The action and source-dependent inverse-vierbein
   equations supply the chosen auxiliary beta0/beta1 model. We explicitly
   name the extra endpoint cosmological terms rather than attribute them
   to the paper's restricted chosen coefficients. The actual matter
   vierbein and its source response must not be replaced by a frozen
   metric-only composite expression. No cutoff, general UV completion, or
   rolling perturbation-health theorem is imported from this source.

2. Capozziello and Martín-Moruno, [*Bounces, turnarounds and singularities in
   bimetric gravity*, arXiv:1211.0214v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.0214),
   equation (15) and equations (18)–(19). This paper derives a proper-velocity
   relation from cosmological Bianchi constraints and relates extrema in
   two dynamical metrics on a regular positive-lapse branch. Its treatment
   excludes specified exceptional parameter/solution branches and permits
   arbitrary hidden-sector matter. The method is relevant prior art; its
   conclusions are not a no-bounce theorem for the present source on a
   third, auxiliary physical metric. Our undivided link equations cover
   zero Hubble rates directly and require neither an infinite hidden
   proper-time tail nor a quotient by a Hubble rate. No general novelty
   assertion is made.

3. Baccetti, Martín-Moruno and Visser, [*Null Energy Condition violations in
   bimetric gravity*, arXiv:1206.3814v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.3814),
   section 4 and the section 5 distinction between interaction and matter
   stresses. The two interaction effective NECs are anticorrelated, apart
   from simultaneous saturation. These are not NEC assumptions on actual
   matter. The present premise is separately and explicitly
   \(n_h=\epsilon(\rho+p)\ge0\) in the actual auxiliary matter metric;
   it is not deduced from that source's interaction-tensor argument.
