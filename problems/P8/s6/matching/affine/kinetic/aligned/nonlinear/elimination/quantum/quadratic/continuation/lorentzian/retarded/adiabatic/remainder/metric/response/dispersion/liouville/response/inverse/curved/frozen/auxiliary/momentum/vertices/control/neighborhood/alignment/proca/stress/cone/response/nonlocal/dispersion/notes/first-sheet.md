# First-sheet gap and absence of zeros

The integral representation defines H analytically off (-infinity,-4m²]. Its positive weight follows from 3-2z+3z²=3(z-1/3)²+8/3. For p=a+ib, the imaginary part of its integrand is exactly

    4m² b W(y) / [(4m²+a(1-y²))²+b²(1-y²)²].

For b nonzero its integral has strictly the sign of b. H therefore has no nonreal zero on the first sheet. On the uncut real interval (-4m²,infinity), differentiation under the integral gives a strictly positive derivative. The direct endpoint value H(-4m²)=16/15 proves the real gap, with H(p)>=4 for p>=0.

For the upper cut put z=1-4m²/tau, tau>4m². Exact continuation of the radial moments gives H(-tau+i0)=D(z)+i pi U(z), where

    D=16/15+z-3z²+sqrt(z)(3-2z+3z²) atanh(sqrt(z)),
    U=sqrt(z)(3-2z+3z²)/2 > 0.

Thus neither open cut bank has a zero. The endpoint again has H=16/15. The infinity behavior is logarithmically growing, not a missing finite pole.

A stronger uniform gap follows directly. On 0<=z<1, atanh(sqrt(z))>=sqrt(z): their difference vanishes at zero and has derivative sqrt(z)/[2(1-z)]>=0. Therefore

    D >=16/15+4z-5z²+3z³
      =16/15+z[3(z-5/6)²+23/12] >=16/15.

The real part of H is harmonic on the slit plane. On both cut banks it is at least 16/15; on a shrinking threshold circle it is at least 16/15-o(1). The explicit uniform large-circle expansion gives real part 2 log(R/m²)-14/15+o(1), above the same gap for large R. The minimum principle on these bounded excised domains, followed by the two limits, proves

    Re H(p)>=16/15,  |1/F(p)|<=15/16

throughout the first sheet. All polynomial, derivative, imaginary-sign and leading-circle identities used here are checked exactly. This analytic gap is for the fixed scalar loop factor, not for the full coupled tree-plus-loop determinant.
