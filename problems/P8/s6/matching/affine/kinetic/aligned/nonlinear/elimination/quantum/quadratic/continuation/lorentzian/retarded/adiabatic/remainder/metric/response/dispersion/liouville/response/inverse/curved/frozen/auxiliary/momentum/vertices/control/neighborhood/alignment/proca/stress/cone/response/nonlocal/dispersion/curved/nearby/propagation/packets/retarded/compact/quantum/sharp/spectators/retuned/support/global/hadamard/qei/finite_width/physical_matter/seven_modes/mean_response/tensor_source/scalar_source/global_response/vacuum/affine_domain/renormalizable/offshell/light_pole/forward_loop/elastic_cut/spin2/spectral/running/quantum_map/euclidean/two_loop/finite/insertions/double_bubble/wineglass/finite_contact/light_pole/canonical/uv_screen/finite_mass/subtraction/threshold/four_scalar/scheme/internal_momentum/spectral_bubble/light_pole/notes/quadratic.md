# One light covariance in the complete quadratic half-trace

The frozen S6.112 calculation writes the full quadratic term as
one half the trace of the light covariance times the actual
quartic-action Hessian. Differentiate D -> D+h P, where P is the
entire first on-shell fermion propagator insertion. The derivative
contains the local quartic tadpole, the stationary-heavy tadpole,
and the mixed term

    -(G^2/2) Tr[P diag(Phi) K_H^-1 diag(Phi)].

A separate reconstruction from the actual Hessian checks the
whole expression, not just the mixed term. All four two-site
external second derivatives independently give the mixed
Euclidean inverse coefficient -G^2 P_ij (K_H^-1)_ij.

There is one light covariance in this half-trace and exactly one
light line in the mixed H-Phi bubble. The heavy line has no direct
fermion Yukawa coupling. There is no factor of two from swapping
two light lines, unlike the quartic outer-bubble family.

Let T_insert denote the whole commonly regulated inserted light
tadpole. The H one-point increment is G T_insert/2; its fixed
counterterm is the negative of this. The stationary-heavy light
mass piece cancels against the induced counterterm, leaving
L T_insert/2, a local mass term. All remaining affine local terms
cancel in the outer mass-one/unit-residue subtraction. Their
separate unregulated values are not bounded or relabeled finite.

This is the entire once-differentiated scalar half-trace. It is
not every enlarged-model quadratic diagram at two loops.
