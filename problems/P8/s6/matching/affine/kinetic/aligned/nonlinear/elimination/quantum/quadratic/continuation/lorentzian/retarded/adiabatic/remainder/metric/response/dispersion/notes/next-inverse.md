# What is still needed for a coupled causal inverse

S6.68 provides the prepared rolling response in C10-to-C0. This
checkpoint supplies an exact flat massive comparator and the
correct regular chart. Neither has yet supplied a closed norm
for solving the full curved metric equations.

A useful published precedent is Meda, Pinamonti and Siemssen,
[Existence and uniqueness of solutions of the semiclassical Einstein
equation in cosmological models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14665),
Section 5. Their scalar-field cosmological existence argument
isolates and causally inverts a logarithmic memory operator to
address derivative loss. It does not cover the present massive
vector with clock-dependent masses and a DHOST lapse constraint.

The project's existing [A.10 inverse proof](../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../../a/applicability/reference/asymptotics/backreaction/response/remainder/residual/existence/notes/inverse.md)
already keeps the logarithmic pole and cut and distinguishes
a continuous inverse from an unjustified C1 endomorphism.
It can be reused as an operator lemma only after deriving the
present matrix operator and its normalization.

The next mathematical steps are:

1. Derive a curved retarded matrix decomposition from the actual
   selected modes, retaining the logarithmic channel, finite local
   complement and initial preparation. Prove a useful no-loss
   estimate for its remainder; matching pole coefficients alone
   does not establish this decomposition.
2. Add the regular classical constraint/evolution block and all
   retained local orders before locating poles or estimating the
   inverse. The tree and matter invariant cannot be inferred from
   the isolated fourth-order comparator.
3. Construct the full retarded inverse on a specified complex
   Laplace contour, or an equivalent causal integral formulation,
   and control the relevant operator norms. Positive real-axis
   invertibility is insufficient for that purpose.
4. Establish compatible data and a solution theorem, then check
   continuation, physical cones and cutoff in the actual theory.
   A short-time inverse by itself is not a global stability result.

The principal approximation's spurious positive-real zero warns
against extrapolating a high-frequency logarithm down to s~m.
Conversely, the exact real-axis gap does not justify deleting any
complex pole, imposing a future boundary condition, or declaring
quantum stability. Such operations change the stated problem.
Original P8's separate finite matching and common-parent V/G/B
obligations remain even after a successful homogeneous inverse.
