# The common-dimensional limit and the boundary of the result

For a fixed smooth prepared source, introduce a real amplitude e.
For sufficiently small |e|, a_m and b_m stay positive. On the fixed
compact time interval the frequency ratio, canonical rate and every
finite WKB coefficient are smooth functions of e, the source jets
and the compactified momentum variables z and t=omega^-2.
The original W8 ratio is uniformly above 1/2. A smaller amplitude
neighborhood therefore keeps it positive uniformly in momentum.

The exact oscillator Cauchy problem has smooth parameter dependence.
The source vanishes on an initial neighborhood, so its physical
Cauchy data and its canonical reference data are exactly unchanged
there. The derivative is the actual causal mode response, not an
independently reselected instantaneous vacuum. The varied physical
readout includes the temporal-constraint contact through the
independent S6.65 Hamiltonian weights and their variations.

The preceding estimates are evaluated quantitatively at e=0.
The same finite coefficient construction in a sufficiently small
amplitude neighborhood gives finite uniform constants with the same
inverse-frequency powers. Thus differentiation may be passed through
the subtracted momentum integral by dominated convergence.

## Dimensional analytic continuation

Use precisely the dimensional initial preparation of S6.55:
the low-order WKB data depend analytically on D, and the fixed Borel
correction and its cutoffs are D-independent. No cutoff is
differentiated with respect to the source or to D.

For a fixed mass and source, take a sufficiently small complex
neighborhood of D=3 inside the old preparation domain. The
D-dependent eighth-order coefficients are finite polynomials in
D with smooth bounded background/source coefficients. Compactness
in u,z,t and positivity at D=3 keep the frequency ratio nonzero
uniformly after shrinking this neighborhood.

The leading omega is D-independent. Its D-dependent frequency
correction is O(omega^-1), so the complex reference phases have
uniformly bounded modulus, rather than exponential growth with
momentum. Analytic plus/minus modes are used; complex conjugation
is imposed only on the real D=3 slice.

The initial off-diagonal mixing coefficients relative to W8(D)
remain O(nu^-6), while the diagonal coefficients remain bounded:
the fixed Borel correction starts at inverse frequency
five, as do the D-dependent sixth-order terms absent from the
fourth-order initial reference. Their coefficient differences and
slopes are uniformly bounded on the chosen dimensional neighborhood.
The old fixed-cutoff tail bound is unchanged. The initial source
derivative is zero in every continued dimension.

The analytic mode equations and their source variations then obey
the same large-momentum power estimates, with possibly larger finite
constants. The Wronskian replaces conjugate normalization by its
analytic plus/minus identity. The slowest subtracted response tail
is nu^-4. With |Re D-3|<1/4, the radial measure leaves an integrable
bound at infinity; compact low momentum is controlled by the regular
canonical equations and their isotropic limit. These facts justify
the common-dimensional finite limit and introduce no additional
state-dependent pole or evanescent term.

This is an existence argument for a sufficiently small complex-D
neighborhood for the fixed mass/source. The displayed numerical
operator bounds are on the physical D=3 slice, not uniform numerical
bounds throughout the whole earlier dimensional strip.

## Finite response and scope

    delta J_ren = integral(delta J_exact-delta J_ad,0..4)
                 + the S6.65 finite local operator

homogeneous prepared sector. The separate finite radial/contact

not added twice and no extra finite Y^2 Wilson value is inferred.

and subtraction operators are local in time. The resulting response
is therefore retarded on this source domain. This does not transfer
ordinary-Proca cone or Hadamard theorems to a finite anisotropic
off-clock operator, and it is not a spatial causal-support proof
