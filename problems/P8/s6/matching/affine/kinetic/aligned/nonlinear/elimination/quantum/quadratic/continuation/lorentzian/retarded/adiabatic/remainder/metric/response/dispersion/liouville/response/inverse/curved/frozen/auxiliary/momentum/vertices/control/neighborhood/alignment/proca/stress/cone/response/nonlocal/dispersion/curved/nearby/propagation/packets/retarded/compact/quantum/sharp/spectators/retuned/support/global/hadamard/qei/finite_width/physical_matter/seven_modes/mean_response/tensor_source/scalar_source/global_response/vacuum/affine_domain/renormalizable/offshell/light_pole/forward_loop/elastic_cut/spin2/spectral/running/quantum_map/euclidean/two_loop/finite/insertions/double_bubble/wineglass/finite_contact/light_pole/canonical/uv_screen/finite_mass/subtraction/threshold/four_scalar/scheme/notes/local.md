# Complete local vacuum, one-point and light-pole references

The independent scalar two-point derivation is the
frozen S6.112 one. Write A=x(1-x) and
Delta=xM+1-x-As. At scale mu=mF,

    T_light=-(ell+1)/Q,
    B_HL(s)=[ell-integral_0^1 log Delta dx]/Q.

The complete scalar Euclidean two-point increment
after fixing the H one-point function to zero is

    f_scalar(s)=(L/2)T_light-g B_HL(s).

To see the one-point cancellation explicitly, the
classical stationary-H reduction gives a local
(L/2-g/(2M))T_light term. The H tadpole is G T_light/2,
so choose its whole local counterterm J_H=-G T_light/2.
The induced term -G J_H Phi^2/(2M) restores
g T_light/(2M) in the light mass, leaving L T_light/2.
This is also the direct full-model 1PI light result.
The same argument keeps regulated and finite pieces.

Before subtraction, the scalar two-point UV reference
is -(L/2+g)Ibar/Q. The complete fermion one from
S6.131 is 2NY(6mF^2-s)Ibar/Q. Their only UV slope
is the already recorded fermion field pole.
Tadpoles and finite mass terms are retained until
the physical mass reference is fixed.

In the convention Pi_scalar=-f_scalar,
r=Pi_scalar'(1) is the positive integral of
(g/Q)A/[xM+(1-x)^2]. The inherited parameter proof
bounds it by g/(288M). The fermion slope fp has
its cancellation-free rational enclosure from S6.131.
Set kappa=1+r-fp>0 and the finite reference mass
to 1+Pi_scalar(1)-f_fermion(1). Then the selected
one-loop quadratic functional, after constant field
normalization, is exactly

    Gamma_can=1-s+[f_fermion,R(s)-Pi_scalar,R(s)]/kappa.

Both remainders have a double zero at one. Their
unit-disc quadratic bound makes the remaining factor
nonzero, giving one mass-one pole with unit residue
and no other pole on this disc. Positive local
potential curvature follows from the same bound
at zero. This is not a global potential theorem.

At the named masses, Delta(1,x) lies between 1 and M:
its lower and upper differences factor into positive
polynomials. Since 1<M<mF^2, its logarithm lies
between zero and ell. Thus the scalar Pi mass anchor
is bounded explicitly by
[L(ell+1)/2+g ell]/Q, below 10^-6 here.
It is kept in the combined large finite mass reference.

At Phi=H=0, the two real scalar vacuum constants are

    V_scalar,0=[-ell-3/2+M^2(log M-ell-3/2)]/(4Q).

Add all fourteen Dirac flavors' 63mF^4/Q and fix the
vacuum-energy reference to minus that total. The
massless gauge/ghost one-loop constant is scaleless
in this regulator; no nonperturbative vacuum-energy
claim follows. A linear H counterterm changes the
vacuum constant only quadratically in its one-loop
coefficient, at later order.

The reference scalar propagator used inside all
one-loop graphs has physical reference mass one.
The very large fixed mass counterterm is not treated
as a negative free mass. Exact normalization here
is of the selected one-loop quadratic functional,
not an all-loop resummation or an omitted-loop bound.
