# Two independent derivations of the finite scheme map

Use one common dimensional regulator and reference scale
mu=mF. The regulated mass-one zero-momentum scalar bubble
has I0=Ibar_MS+ell, ell=log(mu^2). The old scheme subtracts
the whole I0, not just its pole. Its total scalar vertex
counterterms are LG I0/(2Q), g I0/(2Q) and
3L^2 I0/(2Q)+sigma for G,M,L respectively.
Sigma is the fixed S6.125 finite potential contact:

    sigma=6[(-L+g/M)g J1+g^2 J2]/Q,
    J1=M log(M)/(M-1)^2-1/(M-1),
    J2=(M+1)log(M)/(M-1)^3-2/(M-1)^2.

It is not fitted or left as an adjustable new parameter.

For the full MS interaction scheme, the total vertex
poles are LG Ibar/(2Q), g Ibar/(2Q), and
(3L^2/2-24NY^2)Ibar/Q. The last includes the complete
fermion quartic pole; its minimal field factor is
zF=-2NY Ibar/Q. The corresponding bare quartic pole
is obtained by removing two field factors:

    (3L^2/2+4NYL-24NY^2)Ibar/Q.

Its beta-function coefficient is
3L^2+8NYL-48NY^2, exactly the inherited marginal flow.
The bare cubic similarly contains the field-pole term
2NYG Ibar/Q even though the total one-loop fermion
G vertex counterterm is zero. This distinction between
bare parameter and total canonical vertex is essential.

The scalar finite OS field correction is -r; the
fermion one is +fp. Thus Z_star=1+h(zF-r+fp), whereas
Z_MS=1+h zF. Keep the H field convention Z_H=1 rather
than introducing an unprovided stable-heavy OS scheme.

Equate through h the two bare quantities

    G_B=(G_ren+h delta G_total)/Z_phi,
    L_B=(L_ren+h delta L_total)/Z_phi^2,
    M_B=M_ren+h delta M_total.

Using the old scalar total counterterms, the same
fermion quartic pole and the displayed field factors
gives precisely the three star-parameter shifts in
the formulation. The complete finite logarithm,
fixed sigma and both slope signs survive. Every
Ibar cancels in the finite conversion.

Independently, subtract the two total counterterm
amplitudes. Their difference is
ell sum C(z)^2/(2Q)+sigma. Add the finite field
conversion 2(fp-r)A0; this equals literal variation
of the full tree amplitude under all three star
parameter shifts. Neither derivation uses a b2 fit.

The star parameters are a representation of the
specified MS interaction point. They are not asserted
to equal its raw numerical couplings. Counterterms
inserted inside one-loop graphs and changes of
one-loop graphs under these first-order parameter
shifts belong to two loops and are not included here.
