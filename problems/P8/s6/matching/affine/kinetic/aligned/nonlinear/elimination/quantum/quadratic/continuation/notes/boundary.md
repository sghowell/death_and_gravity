# Nonzero finite control for a four-dimensional boundary term

The full scalar heat coefficient contains GB/180, where
GB=Riemann^2-4Ricci^2+R^2. The flat-FLRW geometric identity in
D+1 dimensions is

    GB=D(D-1)(D-2)[4H^2 H'+(D+1)H^4].

With W_D=D_u+D H, this becomes exactly

    GB=(4/3)D(D-1)(D-2) W_D(H^3)
       -D(D-1)(D-2)(D-3)H^4/3.

The first term is a covariant boundary flux. The second has a
simple zero at D=3 and therefore can leave a finite contribution
after multiplication by the dimensional pole. Dropping GB by a
four-dimensional topological identity before continuation
silently selects a different evanescent prescription.

For the scalar-mass probe Y=m^2 f g, on the auxiliary FLRW metric,
the first D derivative of the quadratic -GB/180 density reduces
modulo compact boundaries to

    Q_GB,1=H^2 f'^2/60+H^2(H'+H^2)f^2/30.

It follows by retaining the D-dimensional boundary identity,
then extracting (1/90)[lambda^2](H+v')^4 at D=3, with
v'=lambda f'/2-lambda^2 f f'/2. The exact Euler operator is
nonzero; at H=1,H'=0,f=1,f'=f''=0 it equals 1/15.
The pole's epsilon coefficient is -2 times this D derivative;
the corresponding counterterm's finite component has the
opposite sign.

This is a control of the order of continuation and variation.
It does not delete the bounce's asymptotic fluxes, assert that
different schemes have different physical predictions after
matching, or supply the missing finite causal kernel.
