# Common-dimensional finite limit and scope of the response

For a fixed mass and fixed smooth prepared sources, use the actual
positive mass functions along N=1+e n and a_hat=a exp(e zeta).
A sufficiently small real amplitude neighborhood keeps the lapse,
scale and both mass coefficients positive on the compact interval.
The source vanishes on an initial neighborhood, so the original
physical Cauchy data and every initial comparison-reference jet
are unchanged.

The exact oscillator problem depends smoothly on this parameter.
Its derivative is the retarded mode variation with zero independent
initial response. The finite WKB coefficients depend smoothly on
the source jets through the required finite order. Compactness in
time, compactified momentum z and inverse frequency keeps the
reference ratio nonzero after reducing the amplitude neighborhood.
The coefficient estimates have finite uniform constants with the
same large-momentum powers in this neighborhood. This supplies
dominated convergence for differentiating the subtracted integral.

## The dimensional argument

Use the unchanged S6.55 initial continuation:
W4(D) plus the fixed D-independent Borel correction and cutoffs.
For fixed mass and sources choose a sufficiently small complex-D
neighborhood of three inside the original continuation domain.
Its size is not asserted to be uniform over the whole family of
masses or source profiles.

The leading metric-varied frequencies are D-independent.
The D-dependent reference corrections start at inverse frequency,
so their complex phases have uniformly bounded modulus in momentum
on this compact time interval. The reference ratio remains
nonzero. Analytic plus/minus modes are used; complex conjugation
is imposed only on the physical real slice.

The off-diagonal initial mixing coefficients remain O(nu^-6)
uniformly in this neighborhood, while the diagonal coefficients
stay bounded. The initial source derivative is zero in every
continued dimension. The fixed-cutoff tail is unchanged.
Analytic mode transport and its metric variation retain the same
large-momentum powers. The analytic Wronskian cancels the unmixed
reference contribution.

The slowest varied subtracted tail is nu^-4. For
|Re D-3|<1/4 the radial measure leaves an integrable large-momentum
bound. Low momentum is regular in the canonical equations and
their isotropic limit. The additional volume factor and the
pressure factor 1/D are analytic and bounded near D=3. This
justifies the common finite limit without an extra state-dependent
pole or evanescent assignment.

Only the D=3 slice carries the displayed numerical constants.
They are not uniform numerical bounds throughout the older
dimensional strip. This proof uses the explicit homogeneous
mode family, not a transfer of ordinary-Proca Hadamard or cone
theorems to a finite off-clock anisotropic operator.

## Assemble the actual finite response

In the specified prescription,

    delta T_vector,ren
      = integral(delta T_exact-delta T_ad,0..4)
        + the S6.67 finite physical local response.

The four physical readout tails include the output-normalization
contacts as well as the second mass vertices. The radial finite
part and the counterterm first dimensional jet are combined
before assigning the local finite operator. No zero-order
potential is added twice and no new finite Wilson coefficient
is inferred.

Add the already-fixed S6.60 scalar-action response, holding its
profiles fixed. Its gradient cancels the selected background
vector stress as before; its Hessian does not vanish and is
retained. The resulting bounded two-source operator is the
Gaussian correction about that background-cancelled clock,
not the complete interacting metric dynamics.

Exact mode response is retarded in time; the adiabatic subtraction,
finite counterterms and fixed scalar-action contribution are local
in time. The finite response is therefore retarded on the prepared
source domain.

## What this does not establish

No arbitrary spatial-momentum or independent initial covariance
response, full coupled inverse, nonlinear feedback, quantum
stability/cones, other loops, interaction or cutoff estimate is
proved here. The C10-to-C0 operator has an explicit derivative
loss relative to the low-order metric equations. It cannot be
substituted into a no-loss contraction argument.

The common UV parent, finite Wilson matching and V/G/B
obligations are also still open. Original P8 remains open,
without a user-intervention blocker.
