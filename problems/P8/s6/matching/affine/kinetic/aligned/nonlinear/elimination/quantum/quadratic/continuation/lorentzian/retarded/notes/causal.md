# Initial preparation, causal support and the remaining finite problem

The initial selected data are v0=(2W_B)^(-1/2) and
p0=(-W_B'/[2W_B]-iW_B-d)v0, using exactly the frozen S6.55
time-independent Borel cutoffs. The Wronskian is i and the real
canonical covariance has determinant 1/4. No WKB truncation,
new vacuum or Euclidean preparation replaces that state.

Choose smooth test sources compactly supported strictly after
u0=-1/2 and zero on an initial neighborhood. The original equations
and state agree throughout that neighborhood; consequently this
restricted perturbative response has no independent initial
covariance variation. This is not a claim about arbitrary changes
of the Cauchy metric or the principal longitudinal impedance.
The S6.58 preparation obstruction on a changed initial metric is
not removed.

On the unchanged clock the vector is ordinary positive-mass Proca.
Its causal propagator is a local differential operator applied to
the associated normally hyperbolic one-form propagator, and its
CCR identify the field commutator with that causal propagator.
These precise ingredients are supplied by
[Moretti, Murro and Volpe, Sections 3.2 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.09278v3).
They apply here only on the unchanged ordinary-Proca background.

At spacelike separation the field commutator vanishes. The
commutator of the quadratic currents therefore also vanishes.
Equivalently, the difference of two Wick products factors into
symmetric two-point functions times field commutators. Multiplying
by the retarded time ordering gives background causal support
away from coincidence. Any contact term or finite local
counterterm is supported on the diagonal and cannot enlarge
this off-diagonal support. This statement is about the continuum
distribution, not a finite momentum cutoff, which need not be
microcausal.

Coincident retarded products still require a common regularization
and local extension. The necessity of combining local and commutator
terms is explained in
[Anderson, Molina-Paris and Mottola, Section III](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0209075).
Their worked field example is scalar, not a substituted Proca
calculation. The actual constraint contact, three-polarization
kernel, static check and pole checks above are derived independently.

Neither support nor the pole calculation supplies the specified
finite extension. In particular, it is not legitimate to insert
an independently subtracted finite contact expectation, discard
the dimensional part of the bubble, and then add the S6.63
evanescent counterterm. All components need the same D-dependent
normalization before the finite limit. The full metric and second
mass-source vertices remain outside this mass-only block.

Finally, every finite Taylor coefficient of
delta(x-(1+e)t) at e=0 is supported on x=t, although for e>0
the exact front lies at x=(1+e)t. The code checks the first three
coefficients and the identity holds at every order by the chain
rule. Thus perturbative background-cone support is not a proof
that a finite off-clock background has the same physical cone.
No nonlinear cone, feedback norm, quantum stability or UV
completion claim is made.
