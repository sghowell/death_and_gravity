# Classical characteristic separation

Compare the outgoing principal clock characteristic with a physical
matter null characteristic. Give them the same emission event at
u=-T and compare their comoving positions on the same slice u=T.
The background is spatially homogeneous, with a=eR and dt=Ndu, so

    dx_matter/du=N/(eR),
    dx_clock/du=N c_clock/(eR).

Both characteristics remain in the local homogeneous spacetime;
there is no assumed asymptotic S-matrix, source gluing or incoming
Minkowski region. Their separation is exactly

    Delta x=int[-T,T] N/(eR) (c_clock-1) du.

The inequalities from enclosures.md therefore give, for every
0<T<=10^-7,

    2T (99/100) (2.5*10^-6)
       < Delta x <
    2T (101/100) (8*10^-6).

At T=10^-7 the lower bound is 99/(2*10^14), and the upper
bound is 202/(125*10^12). These are comoving-coordinate distances
in the fixed normalization R(0)=1, not proper lengths or time
delays silently compared at different slices. Multiplication by
the common final physical scale would preserve their sign.

The comparison concerns rays of the derived classical principal
system. It does not yet supply a frequency, packet width,
mode-conversion error, detector observable, finite-band remainder
or a signal resolved below an interacting cutoff. A strictly
positive ray separation alone is not any of those estimates.
