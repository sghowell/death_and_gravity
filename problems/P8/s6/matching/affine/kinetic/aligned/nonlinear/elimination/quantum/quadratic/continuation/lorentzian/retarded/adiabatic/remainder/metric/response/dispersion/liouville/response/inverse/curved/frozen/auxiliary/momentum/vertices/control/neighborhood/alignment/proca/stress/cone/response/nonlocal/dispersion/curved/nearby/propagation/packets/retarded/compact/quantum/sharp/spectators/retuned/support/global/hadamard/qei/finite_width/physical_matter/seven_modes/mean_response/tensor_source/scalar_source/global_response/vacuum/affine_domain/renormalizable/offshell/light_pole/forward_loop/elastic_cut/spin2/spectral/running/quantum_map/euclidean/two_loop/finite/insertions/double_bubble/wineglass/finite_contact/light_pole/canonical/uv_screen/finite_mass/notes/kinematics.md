# Complex on-shell domain and cut continuation

For a real channel invariant s in [1,5], set E=sqrt(s)/2
and p^2=s/4-1. Take incoming scalars (E,0,0,+/-p)
and outgoing gauge particles (E,+/-E n), with n a real
unit three-vector. Continue p to an imaginary value for
s<4 without complex conjugating the external momenta.
The two real spatial transverse polarization vectors have
unit Euclidean norm. Rotational invariance permits n in
the xz plane; a rational stereographic parameterization
checks both polarizations, all mass shells and momentum
conservation. Its missing endpoint is obtained by continuity.

On this domain E<5/4 and |p|<1. A scalar Euclidean
four-vector has component norm E+|p|<3; a gauge vector has
norm E(1+sum|n_i|)<=E(1+sqrt(3))<4. Fix Phi1 at the
first vertex and permute the other three labels: these
are exactly the six cyclic labelled box orders.
Route one propagator with shift zero and the remaining
three with cumulative external momenta. Every shift
contains at most three external vectors, so its component
norm is strictly below 12. All six routes close exactly.

For m>=24, the expansion parameter for every resolvent
is strictly less than 12/m<=1/2. Initially the expansion
agrees with the regularized amplitude at real Euclidean
external momenta. After separating the low-degree terms,
the tail is absolutely and locally uniformly convergent
in this complex domain. It therefore defines its unique
analytic continuation to the stated on-shell compact set.
No heavy propagator singularity occurs in that domain.
This does not translate an integration contour by an
uncontrolled complex momentum.

The range 4<s<5 is a physical open anchor for the scalar
transition and its optical two-gauge cut. Both one-loop
transition factors continue holomorphically in the
external scalar momenta. The angular integral is over
a compact real sphere with the same uniform bound.
Exchanging the two identical scalar legs reverses p
and leaves the complete transition invariant. The
continued cut integral is even in p, hence locally
analytic in p^2 through p=0. Continue from the physical
anchor through s=4 down to [1,3].

Only the gauge particles are put on the two-particle
cut; their phase space remains real and nonzero for
s>0. Below s=4 this expression is an analytically
continued discontinuity, not a positive physical
massive-scalar cross section. In particular, the
continued second transition factor is not defined
by conjugating a complex initial scalar momentum.
The same norm bound applies separately to both factors.
