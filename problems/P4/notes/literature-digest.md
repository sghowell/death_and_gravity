# Literature digest — CAP for the Evans–Coleman CSS radiation-fluid solution and its unstable-mode count

Target: CAP of existence of the Evans–Coleman (EC) CSS solution of spherically symmetric Einstein–Euler, $p=k\rho$, $k=1/3$, plus a validated count of exactly one unstable mode ($\gamma_{\rm PBH}=1/\lambda_1\approx0.3558$).

Abbreviations: HM01 = gr-qc/0101064; H03 = gr-qc/0302004; KHA95 = gr-qc/9503007; KHA99 = gr-qc/9607010; M95 = gr-qc/9504008; EC94 = gr-qc/9402041; GHJ = arXiv:2112.10826; GHJS = arXiv:2509.12435; CCGNU = gr-qc/9901031. Equation numbers are the cited paper's. Inaccessible sources are flagged.

## 1. The CSS equations

### 1.1 KHA formulation (Schwarzschild/polar-areal coordinates; $k=1/3$) — KHA95 §3–4, KHA99 §IV

Metric (KHA95 eq. 16): $ds^2=-\alpha^2(t,r)dt^2+a^2(t,r)dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2$; residual freedom $t\mapsto F^{-1}(t)$ (eq. 17). Fluid $T_{ab}=\rho u_au_b+p(g_{ab}+u_au_b)$, $p=(\gamma-1)\rho$, $u_t=a(1-V^2)^{-1/2}$, $u_r=aV(1-V^2)^{-1/2}$. Similarity variables and unknowns:
$$ s\equiv-\ln(-t),\quad x\equiv\ln(-r/t),\qquad N\equiv\alpha a^{-1}e^{-x},\quad A\equiv a^2,\quad \omega\equiv 4\pi r^2a^2\rho . $$
Autonomous system (KHA95 eq. 18, $\gamma=4/3$):
$$ \frac{A_{,x}}{A}=1-A+\frac{2\omega}{1-V^2}\Big(1+\frac{V^2}{3}\Big),\qquad \frac{N_{,x}}{N}=-2+A-\frac{2\omega}{3}, $$
$$ \frac{\omega_{,s}+(1+NV)\omega_{,x}}{\omega}+\frac{4\{VV_{,s}+(N+V)V_{,x}\}}{3(1-V^2)}-\frac{NVA_{,x}}{3A}+\frac{4VN_{,x}}{3}+2N\Big(1+\frac{4\omega}{9(1-V^2)}\Big)=0, $$
$$ \frac{4V\omega_{,s}+(4V+N+3NV^2)\omega_{,x}}{\omega}+\frac{4\{(1+V^2)V_{,s}+(1+V^2+2NV)V_{,x}\}}{1-V^2}+\frac{N(1-V^2)A_{,x}}{A}+4(1+V^2)N_{,x}+2N(1+3V^2)=0 . $$
CSS solutions: drop all $\partial_s$ terms (KHA95 §4). General-$\gamma$ version: KHA99 eqs. (210a–e), with the algebraic identity (211) $1-A+\frac{2\omega(1+(\gamma-1)V^2)}{1-V^2}=-\frac{2\gamma NV\omega}{1-V^2}$ (momentum constraint), used as a check.

Boundary conditions (KHA95 §4; KHA99 §IV.2): (i) analyticity for all $x\in\mathbb R$; (ii) regular centre $A=1$, $V=0$ at $x=-\infty$. Centre asymptotics (KHA99 eqs. 220–222): $A\sim1+A_{-\infty}e^{2x}$, $N\sim N_{-\infty}e^{-x}$, $\omega\sim\omega_{-\infty}e^{2x}$, $V\sim V_{-\infty}e^{x}$, $A_{-\infty}=2\omega_{-\infty}/3$, $N_{-\infty}V_{-\infty}=-2/(3\gamma)$; the fixed point $(A,NV,\omega,V)=(1,-\tfrac{2}{3\gamma},0,0)$ has one unstable direction, so centre regularity is codimension one (KHA99 §IV.4.1); such solutions have $A\ge1$ everywhere (Lemma 5).

Sonic point (gauge-fixed to $x=0$): "where the velocity of the fluid particle seen from the observer on the constant $x$ line is equal to the speed of sound $1/\sqrt3$" (KHA95 §4); the determinant of the principal part of the fluid equations vanishes (KHA99 eq. 214) and finiteness of $\omega_{,x},V_{,x}$ requires proportional rows (eq. 215, "$af-ec=0$"). Zeroth-order data in terms of $V_0\equiv V_{\rm ss}(0)$, $-1\le V_0\le\sqrt{\gamma-1}$ (KHA99 §IV.3.2):
$$ N_0=\frac{1-\sqrt{\gamma-1}\,V_0}{\sqrt{\gamma-1}-V_0},\quad A_0=\frac{\gamma^2+4\gamma-4+8(\gamma-1)^{3/2}V_0-(3\gamma-2)(2-\gamma)V_0^2}{\gamma^2(1-V_0^2)},\quad \omega_0=\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma-1}(\sqrt{\gamma-1}-V_0)(1+\sqrt{\gamma-1}V_0)}{\gamma^2(1-V_0^2)} $$
(the second root of (214) for $N_0$ gives $A_0<1$ and is discarded). "The equations for the first order expansion coefficients … have two solutions in general" (KHA99 §IV.3.2) — the two analytic branches through the sonic point. Hence "the self-similar solution which is regular at the sonic point is … characterized by a single parameter $V_0$", and matching to the centre discretizes $V_0$ (KHA95 §4).

### 1.2 Ori–Piran / Harada–Maeda comoving formulation — HM01 §II

Comoving metric (HM01 eq. 1) $ds^2=-e^{\sigma}dt^2+e^{\omega}dr^2+R^2d\Omega^2$; Misner–Sharp equations (eqs. 3–7):
$$ m_{,r}=4\pi R^2\rho R_{,r},\quad m_{,t}=-4\pi R^2PR_{,t},\quad \sigma_{,r}=-\frac{2P_{,r}}{\rho+P},\quad \omega_{,t}=-\frac{2\rho_{,t}}{\rho+P}-\frac{4R_{,t}}{R},\quad m=\frac R2\Big[1+e^{-\sigma}R_{,t}^2-e^{-\omega}R_{,r}^2\Big]. $$
Dimensionless unknowns and zooming coordinates (eqs. 9–13): $\eta\equiv8\pi r^2\rho$, $S\equiv R/r$, $M\equiv 2m/r$, $\tau\equiv-\ln(-t)$, $z\equiv r/(-t)$. Integrals (14–15): $e^{\sigma}=a_\sigma(t)z^{4k/(1+k)}\eta^{-2k/(1+k)}$, $e^{\omega}=a_\omega(r)\eta^{-2/(1+k)}S^{-4}$; gauge fixed by $a_\sigma=$const, $a_\omega=1$. With $y\equiv M/(\eta S^3)$ (eq. 21), $'\equiv d/d\ln z$, the CSS system is (eqs. 30–35):
$$ (\ln M)'=\frac{k}{1+k}\big(y^{-1}-1\big),\qquad (\ln S)'=-\frac{1-y}{1+k}, $$
$$ (1-y)^2V_z^2-(k+y)^2+(1+k)^2\eta^{-\frac{2}{1+k}}S^{-6}\Big(1-\frac MS\Big)=0\quad\text{(algebraic constraint, eq. 32)}, $$
$$ V_z\equiv-a_\sigma^{-1/2}z^{\frac{1-k}{1+k}}\eta^{-\frac{1-k}{1+k}}S^{-2},\qquad V_R=-V_z\frac{1-y}{k+y}, $$
$$ (\ln\eta)'=2-\frac{4yV_z^2-(1+k)^2S^{-4}\eta^{-\frac{1-k}{1+k}}}{2(V_z^2-k)}\quad\text{(dependent eq. 35)}. $$
$V_z$ is the velocity of the $z=$const line relative to the fluid, $V_R$ that of the $R=$const line (eqs. 23–24). Regular centre: $y=1/3$ at $z=0^+$ (eq. 22), with expansions (36–41)
$$ M=\tfrac{C_k}{3}(2D)^{\frac{k}{1+k}}z^{\frac{2k}{1+k}}[1+O(z^{\frac{2(1+3k)}{3(1+k)}})],\quad S=C_k^{1/3}(2D)^{-\frac{1}{3(1+k)}}z^{-\frac{2}{3(1+k)}}[\dots],\quad \eta=2Dz^2[\dots],\quad C_k=\frac{3(1+k)}{1+3k}, $$
$D\equiv\tfrac12\lim_{z\to0}z^{-2}\eta=4\pi\rho(t,0)t^2$; gauge $a_\sigma=(2D)^{2k/(1+k)}$ makes $e^\sigma=1$ at the centre (eq. 43); $V_z=-C_k^{-2/3}(2D)^{-1/(3(1+k))}z^{(1+3k)/(3(1+k))}[\dots]$ (eq. 44). Note the non-integer powers: the centre is analytic only in local Cartesian coordinates (H03 §3), and $z^{2(1+3k)/(3(1+k))}$ ($=z^{1}$ at $k=1/3$: $2(1+1)/(3\cdot 4/3)=1$) is the expansion step. Sonic point: $V_z^2=k$ (eq. 45); regularity there requires (eq. 46)
$$ 4yk-(1+k)^2\eta^{-\frac{1-k}{1+k}}S^{-4}=0 . $$

### 1.3 Maison's variables — M95 eqs. (2)–(8)

$ds^2=\frac{r^2}{A^2}\big(\frac{dt^2}{B^2t^2}-\frac{dr^2}{r^2}\big)-r^2d\Omega^2$, $U_{\hat t}=\sqrt{1-v^2}$, $U_{\hat r}=v/\sqrt{1-v^2}$, $\tilde\rho\equiv\sqrt{4\pi}\,r\rho$, $\tau=\ln(-t)$, $\sigma=\ln r-\ln(-t)$, $'=d/d\sigma$, $\dot{}=d/d\tau$:
$$ A'/A=\tfrac12A^{-2}\big(1-A^2-2\tilde\rho\tfrac{1+kv^2}{1-v^2}\big),\quad B'/B=A^{-2}\big(2A^2-1+(1-k)\tilde\rho\big),\quad A'/A=(1+k)\frac{\tilde\rho v}{A^2B(1-v^2)}+\dot A/A, $$
plus two fluid equations (6)–(7); for CSS solutions (drop $\dot{}$) eqs. (3),(5) combine to the constraint $A^2=1-\frac{2\tilde\rho(1+(1+k)v/B+kv^2)}{1-v^2}$ (eq. 8). Regular origin = fixed point $v=\tilde\rho=B=0$, $A=1$ as $\sigma\to-\infty$, one-parameter family in $\rho_0$; generic members "develop a singularity (shock front) at the 'sonic point'"; only a discrete set of $\rho_0$ stays regular (M95).

### 1.4 GHJ comoving/Schwarzschild self-similar formulation — GHJ §2.1, §3

Metric (2.7) $g=-e^{2\mu}d\tau^2+e^{2\lambda}dR^2+r^2\gamma$, $u=e^{-\mu}\partial_\tau$, $p=\varepsilon\rho$; equations (2.12)–(2.16). Ansatz (2.19)–(2.25): $\rho=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau^2}\Sigma(y)$, $r=-\sqrt\varepsilon\,\tau\,\tilde r(y)$, $\mathcal V=\sqrt\varepsilon V(y)$, $y=R/(-\sqrt\varepsilon\tau)$. Unknowns (2.26–2.27) $d:=\Sigma^{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}}$, $w:=(1+\varepsilon)\frac{e^\mu V+\tilde r}{\tilde r}-\varepsilon$; comoving ODEs (2.29–2.30):
$$ d'=-\frac{2(1-\varepsilon)d(d-w)}{(1+\varepsilon)y(e^{2\mu-2\lambda}y^{-2}-1)},\qquad w'=\frac{(w+\varepsilon)(1-3w)}{(1+\varepsilon)y}+\frac{2w(d-w)}{y(e^{2\mu-2\lambda}y^{-2}-1)}, $$
sonic point $y_*^2=e^{2\mu(y_*)-2\lambda(y_*)}$ (Def. 2.1). Closed system in the Schwarzschild self-similar coordinate $x:=\tilde r(y)$, $D(x)=d$, $W(x)=w$ (2.35–2.38):
$$ D'=-\frac{2x(1-\varepsilon)D(W+\varepsilon)(D-W)}{B},\quad W'=\frac{1-3W}{x}+\frac{2x(1+\varepsilon)W(W+\varepsilon)(D-W)}{B},\quad B=D^{-\eta}-\big[(W+\varepsilon)^2-\varepsilon(W-1)^2+4\varepsilon DW\big]x^2,\ \eta=\tfrac{2\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}. $$
Exact solutions: far-field $D_f=(1-\varepsilon)^{-\frac{2}{1+\eta}}x^{-\frac{2}{1+\eta}}$, $W_f=1$; Friedmann $D_F=W_F=1/3$ (2.39–2.40). Regular centre forces $W(0)=1/3$; any solution interpolating Friedmann-like ($x\to0$) and far-field-like ($x\to\infty$) behaviour must cross $B=0$ (IVT, §2.1.3). The $\varepsilon=0$ limit (2.41–2.42) is the isothermal Euler–Poisson system. $B$ factorizes as $B=(1-\varepsilon)(J-xW)(J+2\eta(1+D)x+xW)$ (Lemmas 3.6–3.7).

### 1.5 Local structure of the sonic point (node/saddle) — H03 §3, verbatim

"For $0<k<1$, the ODEs have a singular point which is called a sonic point, at which the relative speed $|V|$ of the fluid element to the curve $\xi=$const is equal to the sound speed $\sqrt k$. … If we require regularity, by which we mean the continuity of first derivatives, the possible solutions make a band structure in the space of all solutions. … If we further require analyticity (Taylor-series expandability), the possible solutions are discretized." "Sonic points are classified into nondegenerate nodes, degenerate nodes, saddles and foci, among which the foci are unphysical. For every physical sonic point, there are two possible directions along which solutions can cross the sonic point, and there are two analytic solutions corresponding to these two directions. For a nondegenerate node, the two directions are called primary and secondary ones. For the former, there are one-parameter family of regular solutions including one of the analytic solutions, while for the latter, another analytic solution alone is possible. For a degenerate node, the two directions are degenerate and there are one-parameter family of regular solutions including the one analytic solution. For a saddle one, all that are regular at the sonic point are the two analytic solutions alone."

"The Evans-Coleman solution exists for $0<k\le1$ (Brady et al. for $k=1$). It crosses a saddle for $0<k\lesssim0.41$, a nondegenerate node along the secondary direction for $0.41\lesssim k\lesssim0.89$, a degenerate node for $k\simeq0.89$ and a nondegenerate node along the primary direction for $0.89\lesssim k<1$." (H03 §3, citing Carr et al. 2001, CQG 18, 303 — not accessed.) So at $k=1/3$ a $C^1$ crossing already implies analyticity; no "merely smooth" branch exists there. CCGNU §III describe the $\alpha_*\approx0.89$ transition as "secondary" $\to$ "dominant" direction through a degenerate node without mentioning the saddle regime; M95 reports "from an attractive node to an attractive focus" at $k_{\max}\approx0.888$ and finds no EC beyond — superseded by Neilsen–Choptuik and Brady et al. (per H03, CCGNU).

## 2. The solution family

- Ori–Piran 1990 (PRD 42, 1068; paywalled, not accessed; content via HM01 §II.2, H03 §3, GHJ §1): for $0<k\lesssim0.4$ a discrete set of solutions analytic at centre and sonic point, plus a band of $C^1$ ones; members labelled by the number of oscillations of $V_R$ (= zeros of $y-1$ on $0<z<\infty$): flat Friedmann (FF) 0, GR Larson–Penston (GRLP, "Ori–Piran solution") 0, GR Hunter (a) (GRHA) 1, GR Hunter (b) 2 (HM01 §II.2). GRLP exists for $0<k\lesssim0.036$, naked singularity for $k\lesssim0.0105$ (HM01 §I). FF: $D=\frac{2}{3(1+k)^2}$ (eq. 47). HM01 Table 1 ($D$): $k=0.01$: GRLP 1.439, GRHA $1.265\times10^3$, GRHB $4.414\times10^4$; $k=0.03$: 1.119, $7.204\times10^2$, $1.650\times10^4$.
- At $t=0$ ($z\to\pm\infty$; HM01 eqs. 54–60): $M,S,\eta$ finite, $y\to1$, $V_R\to V_{R\infty}$; "black hole type" if $V_{R\infty}>0$ (GRLP, GRHB), "repulsive type" if $V_{R\infty}<0$ (GRHA); $V_z\sim-(2D)^{-k/(1+k)}\eta_\infty^{-(1-k)/(1+k)}S_\infty^{-2}z^{(1-k)/(1+k)}$.
- EC = GRHA: "Harada and Maeda (2001) and Maeda and Harada (2001) identified that the Evans-Coleman solution is the general relativistic counterpart of the Hunter (a) solution" (H03 §7); KHA99 §V.8, M95: the analytic solution with exactly one zero of $V$; CCGNU criteria: (1) everywhere analytic including the sonic crossing, (2) $V_R$ has exactly one zero; uniqueness by counting (one-parameter family regular at the centre $\cap$ one-parameter family analytic at the sonic line).
- Numbers at $k=1/3$ (EC94): polar slicing, $\xi=r/C(-t)^n$, $n\simeq1.1485$ ($n=1$ in central proper time); as $\xi\to\infty$ ($t=0$): $a\to1.07$, $m(r)/r\to0.0596$, $\Omega=4\pi r^2\rho\to9.56\times10^{-3}$; $\alpha\sim r^{0.129}$ on $t=0$; $\eta_c=1.018828234$ for their Gaussian family, $\beta\simeq0.36$. KHA99 §V.7.2: $\frac{d\bar N_{\rm ss}}{dx}(0)\simeq-0.35699$ (sonic-point gauge). No tabulated EC profile values ($V_0$, $A_0$, $\omega_0$, $z_{sp}$) at $k=1/3$ were found in accessible sources; HM01 give $R/(-t)\simeq0.26,0.28$ at the sonic point only for FF/GRLP at $k=0.01$.
- Existence range: $0<k\le1$ (H03); KHA99 §VII: EC-type solutions "cease to exist for $\gamma\gtrsim1.889$" in their search; CCGNU: for $\alpha\ge\alpha_*\approx0.89$ Neilsen–Choptuik "demonstrated the existence of a regular critical solution".
- Kink instability (H03 §5, verbatim): "All primary-direction nodal-point solutions, degenerate-nodal-point solutions, and saddle-point anti-transsonic solutions are unstable against the kink mode, while all secondary-direction nodal-point solutions, and saddle-point transsonic solutions are stable against the kink mode." "The Evans-Coleman solution is stable for $0<k\lesssim0.89$ while unstable for $0.89\lesssim k<1$. Moreover, all regular but nonanalytic (collapsing) solutions are unstable." Kink modes: perturbations vanishing inside the sonic point, continuous density, discontinuous density gradient at the sonic point; evolution of the gradient is local and analysed to full order (Harada 2001, CQG 18, 4549 — not accessed).
- Asymptotics beyond the sonic point (CCGNU §III): for $0.28\lesssim\alpha<1$ the critical orbit is "asymptotically Minkowski, class A", ending at an equilibrium point at finite $z$; "the solution is only flat towards null infinity for $\alpha>1/3$" (so $k=1/3$ is marginal).

## 3. The linear perturbation problem

- Ansatz (KHA95 §5): $h(s,x)=H_{\rm ss}(x)+\epsilon h_{\rm var}(s,x)$, $h_{\rm var}=h_p(x)e^{\kappa s}$, $\kappa\in\mathbb C$, for $h\in(N,A,\omega,V)$; gauge $N_{\rm var}(s,0)=0$. Same growth law in H03 eq. (16): $\delta h=\epsilon e^{\kappa\tau}F(x)$; M95 eq. (9): $A=A_{\rm ss}(\sigma)-c(\sigma)(p-p^*)e^{-\omega\tau}$ with $\tau=\ln(-t)$; HM01: $Q_1(\tau,z)=e^{\lambda\tau}Q_1(z)$.
- Eigenvalue ODE: substitute into (18) → linear homogeneous first-order ODEs for $(N_p,A_p,\omega_p,V_p)$ (KHA95 §5; KHA99 §V.2 eqs. 258ff., matrix form with $A_x,B_x,C_x,D_x$). Comoving version HM01 eqs. (84)–(87): $M_1'=-\frac{k}{1+k}\frac{y_1}{y}-\frac{\lambda}{1+k}(M_1+ky^{-1}S_1)$, $S_1'=\frac{1}{1+k}yy_1-\frac{\lambda}{1+k}y(M_1+ky^{-1}S_1)$, an algebraic relation (86) $(V_z^2-k)(1-y)(k+y)y_1=[\dots]M_1+[\dots]S_1$ with $\lambda$-dependent brackets, and $y_1=M_1-\eta_1-3S_1$ (83); the derived equation (87) for $y_1'$ has the factor $(V_z^2-k)$ on the left.
- Boundary conditions: centre — $A_p,V_p$ finite at $x=-\infty$ (KHA95), or in HM01 $y_1(0)=0$, $M_1+3kS_1=0$, one free amplitude $\Delta=\eta_1(0)=\delta\rho/\rho(t{=}{-1},r{=}0)$ (eqs. 90–95); sonic point — "The sonic point becomes a regular singular point for the perturbations" (KHA95); KHA99 §V.4: (1) singularity where $A_xD_x-B_xC_x=0$, identical to the background condition (214); (2) smoothness requires proportionality of rows 3 and 4 → one algebraic relation among $(\bar A_p,\bar N_p,\bar\omega_p,V_p)(0)$; (3) a further algebraic identity; (4) with gauge $\bar N_p(0)=0$ all higher coefficients are fixed by $\bar A_p(0)$; hence "the perturbation solutions which satisfy the analyticity condition (i) are specified by one free parameter $\kappa$", and the centre condition discretizes $\kappa$ (KHA95 §5). HM01: regular density perturbation at $z_{sp}$ ⇔ RHS of (87) vanishes there. M95: "regularity of $\varphi$ at the origin and the sonic point acts as a boundary condition leading to a discrete frequency spectrum".
- Gauge modes: the residual freedom $f(s)=s+\epsilon f_1(s)$ acts as $\tilde h_{\rm var}=h_{\rm var}+f_1h_{\rm ss}'$ ($\bar A,\bar\omega,V$), $+\dot f_1$ for $\bar N$ (KHA99 eq. 257); with $f_1=e^{\bar\kappa s}$ this is an eigenmode for every $\bar\kappa\in\mathbb C$ (eq. 285: "this pure gauge mode forms a one-parameter family"). In the sonic-point gauge $\bar N_p(0)=0$ one survives, at $\bar\kappa=-\frac{d\bar N_{\rm ss}}{dx}(0)\simeq0.35699$ (KHA95 §5; KHA99 §V.7.2); in the origin gauge $\bar N_p(-\infty)=0$ (Maison; KHA99 §VI) at $\kappa=1$ (KHA99 footnote resolving M95's "Note added"). In HM01's comoving gauge: $\lambda=\frac{1-k}{1+k}$ ($=1/2$ at $k=1/3$), $M_1=\frac\Delta2(\ln M)'$, $S_1=\frac\Delta2(\ln S)'$, $y_1=\frac\Delta2(\ln y)'$, from $(-t)\to(-t)-\epsilon\frac\Delta2(-t)^{2k/(1+k)}$ (eqs. 96–103). Brady et al. ($k=1$, §II.4.1): gauge modes at $\lambda\simeq1.000$ and $\simeq0.000$.
- Results at $k=1/3$: $\kappa\simeq2.81055255$, $\beta=1/\kappa\simeq0.35580192$ (KHA95 §5; KHA99 Table 2, "which we believe to be exact to the last digit"); M95 Table 1: $\gamma(1/3)=0.3558$. Shooting exclusion: "no other relevant (or marginal) eigenmodes in the range $0\le{\rm Re}\,\kappa\le15$, $|{\rm Im}\,\kappa|\le14$" (KHA95); a "less complete search" in $-1.5\le{\rm Re}\,\kappa<0$, $|{\rm Im}\,\kappa|<2$ finds "an eigenmode with ${\rm Re}\,\kappa\lesssim-1.4$" (KHA99 §V.7.3). Lyapunov PDE analysis (KHA99 §VI; gauge mode at $\kappa=1$; free boundary at the sonic point "because no information can come in from outside"): at $\gamma=1.3,1.4$ the third mode is real, the fourth/fifth a complex pair; unique relevant mode for $1<\gamma\lesssim1.889$. "We have not found a beautiful argument which can restrict possible eigenvalues" (KHA95). General $k$: M95 Table 1 ($k=0.01$: 0.1143; 0.30: 0.3322; 0.888: 0.8157); KHA99 Table 2 ($\gamma=1.3$: $\kappa=3.00990875$; $1.889$: $1.2244458$); HM01 Table 4: GRHA $\lambda=9.39,8.88,8.75,7.62$ at $k=0.001,0.008,0.01,0.03$, GRHB two modes ($5.02$, $47.1$ at $k=0.01$). Brady et al. ($k=1$): one unstable $\lambda\simeq1.065$.
- RG interpretation (KHA95 §2): $\hat{\mathcal R}_{s_0}:H\mapsto H^{(s_0)}$, $H(x)=h(0,x)$ (eqs. 3–5); CSS solution = fixed point; tangent map $\hat T_{s_0}$ (eq. 6), eigenmodes $\widehat{DT}F=\kappa F$ (eq. 7); relevant ${\rm Re}\,\kappa>0$; with a unique relevant mode, $H^{(s_0)}_{\rm init}\simeq H_{\rm ss}+\epsilon e^{\kappa s_0}F_{\rm rel}$ (eq. 12), choose $\epsilon e^{({\rm Re}\kappa)s_0}=O(1)$ (eq. 13), $M_{BH}\simeq O(e^{-s_0})=O(\epsilon^{1/{\rm Re}\kappa})$ (eq. 14), so $\beta=1/{\rm Re}\,\kappa$ (eq. 15). M95 eq. (11) derives the same from the apparent-horizon condition $A=0$.

## 4. The template proof: GHJS, "Nonlinear stability of the Larson–Penston collapse" (arXiv:2509.12435)

Setting: radial isothermal Euler–Poisson; LP solution $(\hat\rho(y),\hat v(y)=y\hat\omega(y))$ real-analytic on $[0,\infty)$, $\hat\rho(0)>1/3$, $\hat\omega(0)=1/3$, $\hat\omega\to1$, $y^2\hat\rho\to$const (Thm 2.1); unique sonic point $y_*\in(2,3)$, $\hat v^2(y_*)=1$; monotonicity $\hat\rho'<0$, $\hat\omega'>0$ for $y>0$ (eq. 2.11).

Function space: Lagrangian variables $z=r/(T-t)$, flow map $\zeta$, $\mu$; near $z=0$, $\hat\zeta\sim z^{1/3}$, hence $\hat\partial_z=z^{2/3}\partial_z$, $\hat D_z=\partial_z(z^{2/3}\cdot)$; weight $\hat G(z)=1/(\hat\partial_z\hat\zeta)^2$, $\hat G=9+O(z^{2/3})$, $z^{2/3}-\hat G\lessgtr0$ for $z\lessgtr z_*$ (Lemma 2.10); monotone weights $g_j'\le0$. $\mathcal H^m_{m,Z}$ = completion of $\mathcal D^{\rm odd}_Z$ (odd parity at the origin) under $\int_0^Z|\hat{\mathcal D}^{m+1}\theta|^2\hat Gg_j+\int_0^Z|\hat{\mathcal D}^m\phi|^2g_j$ (+ small $\beta\times$ low-order term); $\mathcal H^m_{m,Z}\cong H^{m+1}_{\rm odd}\times H^m_{\rm odd}$, measure $y^2dy$, $y\simeq z^{1/3}$ (Def. 2.11, eq. 2.48); $\mathfrak m\ge2$ (Def. 2.14).

Mode stability (Thm 3.4): "For any $Z>z_*$ and $m\ge2$ the operator $\mathbf L:D(\mathbf L)\subset\mathcal H^{2m}_Z\to\mathcal H^{2m}_Z$ is mode stable, i.e. it has exactly one eigenvalue with non-negative real part given by $\lambda=1$." Trivial mode: time translation, eigenfunction $\propto(\hat\zeta,\,z\partial_z\hat\zeta-\hat\zeta)$ (Lemma 3.2); handled by Riesz projection $\mathbf P$, $\|e^{s\mathbf L}(\mathbf I-\mathbf P)\|\le e^{-\Lambda_0 s}$ (Thm 3.3), and nonlinearly by choosing the collapse time $T$ via Brouwer's fixed point theorem (Prop. 8.2). Note $\lambda=1$ is the exact analogue of the gauge/time-shift modes of §3.

Eulerian eigenvalue ODE (Lemma 3.5, eq. 3.31/3.35), $w(y):=1-\hat v^2=1-y^2\hat\omega^2$ (signed distance to the sonic point):
$$ \psi_\lambda''+\Big(\frac2y-\frac{\hat\rho'}{\hat\rho}+\frac{w'}{w}+\frac{2(1-\lambda)\hat v}{w}\Big)\psi_\lambda'+\frac{2\hat\rho+2(1-\lambda)\hat v'-(2-\lambda)(1-\lambda)}{w}\psi_\lambda=0 . $$
Singular points: origin (Frobenius, parity) and sonic point (regular singular). Lemma 3.6: any eigenfunction with ${\rm Re}\,\lambda\ge0$ in $\mathcal H^{2m,{\rm Eul}}_Z$ is real-analytic on $[0,y_*]$ with $\psi_\lambda=y\sum A_jy^j$ at 0 and $\psi_\lambda=\sum B_j(y-y_*)^j$ at $y_*$; the second Frobenius solution at $y_*$ behaves like $w^{-(\lambda-1)/(1-1/y_*)}$ (proof of Lemma 3.17), which the Sobolev requirement excludes. So the space of admissible local solutions is one-dimensional at each end.

Exclusion of ${\rm Re}\,\lambda>1$ (§3.3): supersymmetric reformulation $v_\lambda=f_\lambda\psi_\lambda$, $f_\lambda=y\,w^{\frac12-\frac12\frac{1-\lambda}{1-1/y_*}}/(\sqrt{\mathcal A}\sqrt{\hat\rho})$ (Lemma 3.7); energy identity $\int_0^{y_*}(|v_\lambda'|^2+\tilde V|v_\lambda|^2)dy=-(1-\lambda)^2\int_0^{y_*}\frac{|v_\lambda|^2}{w}dy$ forces $(\lambda-1)^2\in\mathbb R$, hence $\lambda\in\mathbb R$ (Lemma 3.8); a quotient argument against the $\lambda=1$ eigenfunction excludes $\lambda>1$ (Lemma 3.9). Threshold: ${\rm Re}\,\lambda\le1$.

High frequency (Prop. 3.10, 3.14): commuting with $(\partial_y(\partial_y+\frac2y))^m$ shifts the first-order coefficient by $2m\frac{w'}{w}<0$ ("damping"); with $m=2$, $\Psi_\lambda=(\partial_yD_y)^2\psi_\lambda$ satisfies an energy inequality $-\int\chi|D_y\Psi_\lambda|^2+\int|{\rm Im}\lambda|^2\chi H_\lambda|\Psi_\lambda|^2\ge\int\chi\tilde H_\lambda|\Psi_\lambda|^2$ (eq. 1.15) with $H_\lambda\le-c<0$; the property of the profile used is the monotonicity (2.11) (giving $w'/w<0$ inside the cone and $\partial_z\hat G\le0$, Lemma 2.10, for dissipativity); interval arithmetic (Lemma D.8) certifies $H_\lambda+\frac{1+b^2/\alpha_*^2}{4}w|D^{(4)}_2|^2<0$ on $[0,y_*]$ for $b=|{\rm Im}\lambda|\ge8$, $a=1-{\rm Re}\lambda\in[0,1]$. Low frequency $|{\rm Im}\lambda|\le1/5$ (Prop. 3.15): quotient $Q_\lambda=\psi_\lambda/g_1$ against the trivial eigenfunction, energy identity plus IA Lemma D.9.

Intermediate strip (Prop. 3.16; $a\in[0,1]$, $b\in[\tfrac15,8]$): sonic-point Taylor expansion with certified growth $|\psi_k/\psi_0|\le4y_*^2\mathfrak C^{k+1-\alpha}/k^3$, $\mathfrak C=8+\tfrac32b$, $\alpha=1.98$, hence an explicit tail bound for $|y/y_*-1|\le\delta<1/\mathfrak C$ (Prop. D.10(i)); odd origin expansion $y^2\psi_\lambda=\sum\tilde\psi_{2k+1}y^{2k+1}$, $|\tilde\psi_{2\ell+1}|\le7\mathfrak C_0^{2\ell-\alpha_0}/(2\ell+1)^3$, $\mathfrak C_0=2+b/4$, $\alpha_0=1.95$ (D.10(ii)); VNODE-LP integrates from $y_*-\delta$ to $y=0.01$ and the two complex vectors $(\psi,\psi')$ are verified linearly independent over a box subdivision of $(a,b)$ (`intermediate_evalue_excluder`; ~100 boxes in main(), Taylor orders $N=n=200$, $\delta=0.08$). "VNODE-LP is not adapted to handling ODE problems with singularities, and so we are forced to build a rigorous approximation close to the singular points" (App. D). The LP profile is validated likewise: $|\rho_j|,|\omega_j|\le C^{j-\alpha}/j^2$, $(C,\alpha)=(8.25,1.95)$ for $y_*\in[2,3]$, $(7.2,1.98)$ for $y_*\in[2.34,2.342]$ (Prop. D.1); $\bar y_*\in[2.3411172805,2.34111728062]$ (Lemma D.6, bisection on the sign of $\omega'$ near the origin); $\hat\rho(0)\in[0.83290803,0.83290811]$ (Lemma D.7). Run "on a MacBook Air 2013".

Public code (github.com/mrischrecker/Larson-Penston-Stability): 3-line README, a `makefile` linking VNODE-LP (filib config), and one C++ file `sonic_Taylor.cc` (7415 lines): interval/complex helpers and box bisectors; LP Taylor construction at sonic point and origin with error bounds; VNODE-LP/FADBAD ODE solvers; bisection solvers for $y_*$, $\rho_0$; eigenfunction Taylor data at both singular points ($\lambda=1-a-ib$ as real/imaginary pairs), `psila_solver`, `intermediate_evalue_excluder`; an "Energy" section evaluating LP derivatives to order 5 (`large_b_*_Prover`, `small_b_*_Prover`); `main()` with every lemma's call.

## 5. The existence template: GHJ, "Naked singularities in the Einstein–Euler system" (arXiv:2112.10826, Ann. PDE 2023)

Theorem 1.1: "For any sufficiently small $0<\varepsilon\ll1$ there exists a radially symmetric real-analytic self-similar solution to the Einstein-Euler system with a curvature singularity at the scaling origin and an outgoing null-geodesic emanating from it all the way to infinity" (RLP spacetime); Theorem 1.2: asymptotically flat naked-singularity solutions by flattening in double-null gauge (§9).

Sonic-point analysis (§2.2, §4): rescale $z=x/x_*$; expansions $D=\sum D_N(x-x_*)^N$, $W=\sum W_N(x-x_*)^N$ (2.43). Order 0: $D_0=W_0$ and $B(x_*)=0$ reduce to $h(\varepsilon,W)=(1+3\varepsilon)W^2+4\varepsilon W+\varepsilon(\varepsilon-1)W^\eta-x_*^{-2}=0$, solved by the IFT near $W=1/x_*$ for $|\varepsilon|\le\varepsilon_0$, $x_*\in[3/2,7/2]$ (Lemma 4.1). Order 1: $W_1$ is a root of a cubic $P(X)=X^3+\frac{a_2}{a_3}X^2+\frac{a_1}{a_3}X+\frac{a_0}{a_3}$ (Lemma 4.7); one "ghost" root $W_1^{gh}=-1/x_*+O(\varepsilon)$ is factored out (Lemma 4.9); the remaining quadratic $Q$ has two real roots for $x_*>x_{\rm crit}(\varepsilon)=2+O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$ (Lemma 4.10). Newtonian limit (4.204–4.205): LP-type $(R_1,W_1)=(-\frac1{x_*},1-\frac2{x_*})$, Hunter-type $(1-\frac3{x_*},0)$, coinciding at $x_*=2$. Branch choice: "we select $W_1=W_1(\varepsilon)$ to correspond to the choice of the branch converging to the LP-type coefficient as $\varepsilon\to0$ … a de facto selection principle" (Def. 4.11, RLP-type). All $(D_N,W_N)$, $N\ge2$, then follow from a recursion $A_N(D_N,W_N)^T=(S^1_N,S^2_N)^T$ with invertible $A_N$ (Hadžić survey 2306.02445 §3), and Theorem 4.18 gives a local real-analytic solution for all $x_*$ in the sonic window $[x_{\min},x_{\max}]\subset[2,3]$ ($x_{\max}$: $W_0=1/3$, the Friedmann value; $x_{\min}=2+\delta_0$), with $N$-dependent growth bounds (App. A).

Shooting parameter: $x_*$. "Friedmann connection" (§5): split the window into $X_{>1/3}$ (solutions staying above $W=1/3$) and $X_{1/3}$ (crossing it), Def. 2.2; continuity plus the monotonicity lemma (Lemma 3.7, an exact first-order equation for $f=J[x;D]-xD$ with signed source) propagate $B>0$, $D>W$ leftward and $B<0$, $D<W$ rightward, excluding a second sonic point; a boundary point $\bar x_*$ reaches $x=0$ with $W(0)=1/3$. Far-field connection (§6, $W\to1$), maximal analytic extension (§7), causal structure (§8).

What is perturbative in $\varepsilon$ and why it stops at $k=1/3$: the sonic data $(W_0,W_1)$ are IFT continuations of the Newtonian roots (Lemmas 4.1, 4.6); the sonic window, the invariant sets $W<1/3$ and $W>\frac{1}{2-2\eta}=\frac12+O(\varepsilon)$ (Lemmas 5.9, 5.12), $x_{\rm crit}(\varepsilon)$, and all a priori bounds are stated for $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_0]$ with $\varepsilon_0$ unspecified. The authors: "Even though the smallness of $\varepsilon$ is crucial for the validity of our estimates, we emphasise that we do not use perturbation theory to construct the relativistic solution" (§2.1.3); "We are aware of no general ODE theory for global existence in the presence of singular sonic points" (§1). Hunter-type branch: "the existence of Hunter-type solutions is not pursued in our work [40]. … the Hunter family of solutions provides a link to the criticality theory for relativistic gases" (Hadžić 2306.02445 Remark 3.6). For EC at $k=1/3$ the needed object is the other (Hunter-type) root of $Q$, a value $\varepsilon=1/3$ far from $0$, and (per §1.5) a saddle-type sonic point rather than the nodal type of GRLP (H03: Ori–Piran solution crosses "a nondegenerate node along the primary direction for $0.036\lesssim k<1/3$").

## 6. Other CAP templates (one paragraph each)

- Buckmaster–Cao-Labora–Gómez-Serrano, arXiv:2208.09445 (smooth imploding Euler): autonomous $2\times2$ system with sonic point $P_s$; the smooth solution through $P_s$ is a "Frobenius-like series expansion" with recursion $Z_n=F_Z/(n-k(r))$ (eq. 1.17), singular when $k(r)\in\mathbb N$ (resonances drive the shooting in $r$); "the first 10000 coefficient pairs $(W_j,Z_j)$ at $r=r_*$ with rigorous error bounds" (~14 h, one CPU); interval arithmetic in Arb (App. B), barrier functions, branch-and-bound; they note GHJ "apply similar arguments in the context of non-autonomous ODEs". Closest template for certified sonic-point Taylor data plus shooting.
- Chen–Hou, arXiv:2305.05660 (Part II): MATLAB + INTLAB; approximate space–time solutions of the linearized equations "with rigorous error control", finitely many stability conditions verified on HPC; no sonic point, but a template for certifying constants of a non-self-adjoint linearized operator via finite-rank approximation.
- Biernat–Donninger, arXiv:1610.09496 (harmonic map heat flow, $d=3$): Chebyshev approximation with rational coefficients, extended to an exact profile by a fixed-point argument; rational-function bounds by interval arithmetic; spectral stability of the self-adjoint linearization by Sturm–Liouville zero counting. Angerer–Kistner–Schörkhuber, arXiv:2607.27072 ($d\in\{3,\dots,6\}$): exact rational arithmetic with "an exact truncated Chebyshev expansion with rigorous tail bounds" (§5), $\sigma(L_{f_0})\subset(-\infty,0)\cup\{1\}$; code at git.uibk.ac.at/csaw4329/hmhf_paper_code. The "certified global approximation + a posteriori fixed point" alternative to Taylor-plus-ODE-solver.
- Sandine, arXiv:2310.18870, 2508.05817 (Hunter-type Euler–Poisson profiles): non-computer-assisted, perturbative; only large-index profiles ("a strict subset of the family … discovered numerically by Hunter"), so Hunter (a), the Newtonian EC analogue, is not covered; builds on GHJS arXiv:2107.12056, which "used rigorous interval arithmetic to control the signs of certain quantities".

## 7. Open-question list for the formulation

(a) Definition of "the EC solution". Candidate: the CSS solution of §1.2 (or §1.1) with (i) regular centre ($y(0^+)=1/3$, analytic in Cartesian coordinates), (ii) analytic crossing of its unique sonic point, (iii) exactly one zero of $V_R$ (CCGNU; KHA99 §V.8). At $k=1/3$ the sonic point is a saddle (H03), so (ii) may be replaced by $C^1$ regularity; the branch is the first-order sonic root that is not the LP-type one (GHJ Lemmas 4.7/4.9; KHA99 "two solutions"). Continuation: through the supersonic region to $t=0$ ($\xi\to\infty$: $a\to1.07$, $m/r\to0.0596$, EC94; $y\to1$ in HM01 variables), and by analogy with GRHA at small $k$ "repulsive type" beyond — unverified at $k=1/3$. PBH domain: the exponent depends only on the eigenproblem on the closed backward sound cone $[0,x_{sp}]$ (KHA integrate from the sonic point to the centre; KHA99 §VI uses a free boundary there since "no information can come in from outside"); the exterior only matters for EC as an intermediate attractor of asymptotically flat data (EC94). Certifying the exterior up to $t=0$ is a formulation choice.

(b) Function space. Options: (1) KHA's analytic class in the gauge $\bar N_p(0)=0$ — the theorem would read: the only eigenvalues with ${\rm Re}\,\kappa\ge0$ are $\kappa_1\approx2.81055$ and the gauge value $\approx0.35699$; (2) a weighted $H^{2m}$ space on $[0,Z]$, $Z>x_{sp}$, à la GHJS, with Frobenius analysis (their Lemma 3.6) turning Sobolev regularity into analyticity at the sonic point — requires the $\lambda$-dependent second Frobenius exponent for the EC linearization (cf. $w^{-(\lambda-1)/(1-1/y_*)}$). Kink modes are excluded by either space, and EC is kink-stable for $k<0.89$ (H03). Nonspherical modes out of scope (Gundlach 2002, per H03: stable for $1/9<k\lesssim0.49$).

(c) Gauge and constraints. The system carries an algebraic identity (KHA99 (211); HM01 (83),(86)) and a regular-singular sonic point; residual gauge freedom yields a pure-gauge eigenmode for every $\bar\kappa$ (KHA99 eq. 285), so the CAP must fix a gauge and identify the single surviving gauge eigenvalue (0.35699, 1, or $(1-k)/(1+k)=1/2$ by gauge) as non-physical, as GHJS isolate $\lambda=1$; certify it by matching its eigenfunction to $h_{\rm ss}'$.

(d) A priori $|\lambda|$ bound. None known for EC (KHA95: "we have not found a beautiful argument which can restrict possible eigenvalues"); numerics cover $0\le{\rm Re}\,\kappa\le15$, $|{\rm Im}\,\kappa|\le14$. GHJS's ${\rm Re}\,\lambda\le1$ and $|{\rm Im}\,\lambda|\le8$ both use LP monotonicity $\hat\rho'<0$, $\hat\omega'>0$; EC has collapsing interior and expanding exterior ($V_R$ changes sign), so a different large-$|\lambda|$ argument (WKB/Frobenius-index incompatibility as $|\lambda|\to\infty$ on $[0,x_{sp}]$) is needed. KHA99 Lyapunov data (next modes real then a complex pair, ${\rm Re}\,\kappa\lesssim-1.4$) suggest an $O(1)$ gap; the compact rectangle would then be certified by a shooting/Wronskian test.

(e) 2024–2026 work. Nothing found treating EC existence at $k=1/3$ or its mode count rigorously (19 Semantic Scholar citations of 2112.10826, 1 of 2509.12435, none relevant). Related: GHJS 2509.12435; Sandine 2310.18870, 2508.05817; Alexander–Hadžić–Schrecker 2311.18795 (non-isentropic Euler–Poisson); Jang–Liu–Schrecker 2310.18483, 2403.12247 (shocks); Shao–Wei–Zhang 2403.11471 (relativistic Euler implosion, no gravity); Zheng–Singh 2605.16235, 2605.16095 (nonlinear stability of Christodoulou's CSS Einstein–scalar naked singularities in $C^{1,\alpha}$); Cicortas–Kehle 2412.09540 (DSS Einstein–scalar); Li–Zhu 2505.20766 (physics); Hadžić 2306.02445 (Remark 3.6: Hunter-type existence open). No "Kim" coauthored Einstein–Euler CSS stability paper surfaced.
