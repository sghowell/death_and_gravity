# Uniform physical bound and an explicitly limited spectral moment

Let r=beta^2,y=x^2,A=1-r>0,B=r(1-y)>=0,D=A+B=1-ry<=1.
The same/opposite helicity amplitudes in units s/(4kappa) are
u=A^2/D and v=B^2/D. Since u+v=D-2AB/D<=D<=1,
u^2+v^2<=1. Including the two copies of each physical helicity gives

    0<Im A(s,0)<=s^2/(256pi kappa^2), s>=4m^2.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the full helicity/phase-space inner
product also gives |Im A(s,z)|<=Im A(s,0) for real physical z.
The complete relative phases, not only their forward magnitudes, are
required for this statement.

Only for4m^2<=a<b, define the positive PHYSICAL WINDOW MOMENT

    J[a,b]=(2/pi)integral_a^b ds Im A_gg(s,0)/(s-2m^2)^3.

The majorant integrates exactly to

    J[a,b]<= [H(b)-H(a)]/(128pi^2kappa^2),
    H(s)=log(s-2m^2)-4m^2/(s-2m^2)-2m^4/(s-2m^2)^2.

The derivative, composition and zero-width identities are checked.
For the original mass1,a4,b10^196, log10<3 and pi^2>1 give the strict
upper bound591/(128kappa^2). Dividing by the unchanged reference
4lambda=4*10^-600 gives less than10^-990. Lambda is a comparison scale,
not a canceled bare operator restored to the current heavy-parent action.

This is NOT the full b20 coefficient: the unphysical low interval below
4m^2, transfer-channel terms, unknown local polynomials, other species,
higher loops and the UV contour are not included. The upper integration
limit does not establish that the EFT is valid to that physical energy.
The estimate controls a formal coefficient on a named interval, not a
total finite-gravity truncation or matching error.
