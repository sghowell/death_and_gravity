## 3. Positive all-spin comparison from overlapping balls

```text

phi(q)=0 for q>1,       psi(q)=q phi(q).
```

For a ball B of radius 1/2, phi(|p-p'|) equals the volume of


for **every spin and every s>=32**. This is a continuum Hilbert-space

Thus the comparison integral

```text

            integrated against psi(q)dq on [0,1], divided by (s-2)^3
```

is nonnegative whenever the stated spectral integral exists. This is
not yet A_E: its integration endpoint and crossed denominator differ.

## 5. Why exact positivity at the finite angular gap fails

At fixed s, an integrable smearing with nonzero even part, supported
strictly away from the forward and backward angular endpoints, cannot
have every allowed even-spin Legendre coefficient nonnegative. Evenize its
angular density f(x). Its support is still separated from both endpoints. The Abel-Poisson
kernel identity is

```text
sum_l (2l+1) r^l P_l(x)=(1-r^2)/(1-2rx+r^2)^(3/2),  0<r<1.
```

Its even part tends uniformly to zero on the support of f as r tends to
one. Thus the Abel sum of f's even Legendre coefficients tends to zero.
If every coefficient were nonnegative, a single positive coefficient
would give a strictly positive limiting lower bound. All coefficients
would therefore have to vanish, forcing f=0 by completeness. This is a
contradiction for a nonzero evenized smearing.

For the present forward-supported weight and s>=32, evenization cannot
cancel it: the forward and reflected supports are disjoint. The argument
also applies when multiplying it by the bounded crossed denominators.
It excludes **exact all-spin positivity of that finite-gap kernel**, not
the approximate inequality of section 4 or all finite-resolution methods.

The exact Fraction calculation makes the issue visible at the actual
E=1/256: for s=32 the truncated comparison kernel is positive at l=128
but negative at l=256 and l=512. All three zero-endpoint comparison kernels
are positive. Checking only a finite low-spin grid would miss this.

This agrees with the distinction in [Bellazzini et al., equations
5.9-5.17](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13780v2#S5): their positive UV kernel
extends to q=0 while the physical IR arc retains finite resolution and
requires finite-coupling errors. Our massive kernel and bound are derived
here; their massless numerical example is not imported.
